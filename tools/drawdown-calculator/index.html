Whichever room is smaller is the one that actually governs your day. That is the whole answer to "daily or total?" — it is not a choice; it is whichever runs out first, and it changes as the floor moves.

Static vs. trailing — the 30-second version

Static floor: your breach line is set on day one (e.g. $90,000 on a $100K account) and never moves. Profits genuinely add room.

Trailing floor: your breach line follows your best balance up. On a $100K account with 10% trailing drawdown, the day your balance peaks at $106,000, your floor quietly becomes $96,000 — you no longer have $10,000 of room to the start, you have $10,000 of room below your peak. Fall back to $100,000 and you have already used 40% of your total room without ever dipping below your starting balance.

EOD vs. intraday: an end-of-day floor trails your best closing balance. An intraday floor trails your best equity — including open, unrealised profit that later disappears. The same winning morning that lifts an intraday floor can leave you with less room by lunch. If your firm counts open P&L, a trade that was up $2,000 and closed flat has still moved your floor.

Why the lock matters: some firms stop the floor from rising once your balance passes a set gain (often the starting balance). Above that point the floor behaves like a static one — set the lock % above and the calculator shows the difference.
