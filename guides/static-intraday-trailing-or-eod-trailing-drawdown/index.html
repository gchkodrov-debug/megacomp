Is the floor static, intraday trailing, or EOD trailing — and does open P&L count?

There is no universal answer, because firms use all three floor types — and two challenges advertising the same headline drawdown percentage can leave you with completely different room. What decides it is four settings in your firm's rulebook: the floor type, whether it is measured on balance or equity, whether open profit and loss counts, and when it resets. Here is what each setting means, in plain words, so you can find yours and write it down.

What does a static floor mean?

The floor is a fixed level for the whole challenge. It does not move when you make profit, so the dollar distance between your account and the floor only changes when your account itself moves.

What does an intraday trailing floor mean?

The floor follows the highest point your account reaches during the day (often the equity peak). A strong morning that fades can therefore leave you with less room in the afternoon than the headline number suggested at the open.

What does an EOD trailing floor mean?

The floor only adjusts at the end of the day, based on the closing level. Intraday spikes up do not move it — it steps up (or stays) once per day, when the day closes.

Does open P&L count?

If your firm measures drawdown on equity, yes — open positions move the number live, including floating losses. If it measures on balance, only closed trades count. Look for the words “balance-based” or “equity-based” in your rules; this single setting changes what “room” means during a trade.

What should I write down before trading?

  • Floor type: static, intraday trailing, or EOD trailing.
  • Measured on balance or equity.
  • Open P&L counts: yes or no.
  • When the daily limit resets, and in which timezone.
  • Today's exact floor levels — daily and total.
