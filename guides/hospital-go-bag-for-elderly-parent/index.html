What should I pack in a hospital go-bag for my elderly parent?

“Is the hospital bag packed? Just in case?” is a worry caregivers voice out loud — because a sudden trip to hospital never gives you time to gather things calmly. Pack the bag before you need it: one bag by the door with your parent's essentials, and a paperwork folder that answers the questions every admissions desk asks.

The paperwork folder (keep it in the bag)

  • Copies of ID and insurance cards.
  • Current medication list, with doses.
  • Conditions and allergies list.
  • Doctors' names and phone numbers.
  • A copy of the advance directive / healthcare power of attorney.
  • Your own contact details, and the next family contact after you.

For your parent

  • Glasses, and hearing aids with spare batteries.
  • Denture case, if they use one.
  • Phone and charger.
  • A change of clothes and underwear, and something warm.
  • Basic toiletries.
  • Slippers with backs, or their usual indoor shoes.

For you

  • A notebook and pen — names, instructions and times get forgotten under stress.
  • Your phone charger.
  • Water and a snack; hospital waits are long.
  • A short list of who to call, in order.

Where should it live?

By the front door or in the car — and tell another family member where it is. Refresh the medication list every time a prescription changes; a bag with last year's list is only half packed. The paperwork folder is the same one that anchors the full caregiver binder.
