Do most traders break prop firm rules on purpose — or just not realise the rule existed?

This question was asked by traders discussing a one-sided-betting rule — some had been caught by it, and the argument was whether anyone breaks these rules deliberately. The dangerous breaches are usually the second kind: not cheating, but a rule buried in the fine print that the trader never read as applying to them. You cannot follow a rule you never wrote down. This page lists the categories that catch people out, and the simple routine that prevents it.

Which rules catch people out?

Read your own firm's list — but these are the categories that repeatedly surprise traders mid-challenge:

  • News-trading windows — restrictions on trading around major news releases, sometimes minutes either side.
  • Weekend and overnight holding — some challenges forbid holding positions over the weekend or overnight.
  • Size limits — maximum contracts or lots, sometimes per instrument.
  • One-sided betting and hedging rules — limits on concentrating trades in one direction, or on hedging the same instrument.
  • Consistency rules — limits on how much of your profit can come from a single day or trade.
  • Inactivity rules — a minimum trading frequency; accounts can be closed for going quiet.

And the related trap from traders' own reports: a rule described as “allowed” in marketing but punished under the fine print. The written rules are the deal — see our guide on comparing firms before you buy.

How do I protect myself?

  1. Before the first trade, read the firm's full rule list once, slowly.
  2. Copy every rule onto a one-page rule sheet for that firm, in your own words, with the firm's numbers filled in.
  3. Tick each rule against how you actually trade — the mismatches are your risk list.
  4. Re-check the sheet weekly, and whenever the firm announces a change.
  5. Keep the dated sheet with your other records — it is part of your evidence folder.
