How do you organize all your family's important documents?

This is one of the most-asked organisation questions online, and the answer that works is boring on purpose: one home for everything, sorted by category, with a one-page index at the front that says what is where — and one trusted person who knows where the binder lives. No app, no system to learn. Here is the setup, step by step.

What goes in it? The category checklist

  • Identity: birth certificates, passports, marriage certificate, Social Security cards.
  • Home: deed or lease, mortgage details, home insurance policy.
  • Money: a list of bank and investment accounts (institutions and account types — not passwords), debts, recent tax returns.
  • Legal: will, powers of attorney, advance directives, any trust papers.
  • Medical: insurance cards and policy numbers, medication lists, key doctors.
  • Vehicles and other policies: titles, registrations, insurance.
  • Digital life: a note of which online accounts exist and where access details are stored — so nothing is unfindable.

How do I set it up?

  1. Gather everything into one pile — drawers, email attachments, the glovebox, all of it.
  2. Sort the pile by the categories above.
  3. Give each category its own section or folder in one binder or box.
  4. Write the index page: category, what is in it, and where the originals are if the binder holds copies.
  5. Tell one trusted person where it lives.
  6. Review it once a year — new accounts, new policies, changed wishes.

Originals or copies?

Both, deliberately: originals of legal documents belong somewhere safe and consistent; the binder holds copies plus a note of where each original is. The index is what turns a pile of “somewhere safe” into something your family can actually use on a bad day.
