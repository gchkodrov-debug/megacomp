Do you size from the daily limit or the total one?

Both limits are running at the same time, every day of the challenge. The daily limit is the most the account can drop in one day; the total limit is the most it can drop across the whole challenge. On any given day, the limit that actually governs you is whichever one leaves you less room — so the working habit is to work out your remaining room under both, before the first trade, and plan from the smaller number. This page is about the records side of that question: what the two numbers are, and how to keep them in front of you daily.

What is the daily limit?

The daily limit (firms also call it the daily loss limit or daily drawdown) is the maximum drop the account is allowed in a single day before the challenge is failed. It is measured against a floor level for that day and resets when a new day starts — room you had yesterday is not room you have today.

What is the total limit?

The total limit (max drawdown) is the maximum drop allowed across the whole challenge, measured against a floor set by the firm. It does not reset daily. Depending on the firm, that floor can be fixed or can move — see our guide on static vs trailing floors.

How do I work out today's room?

  1. Note today's starting balance (and equity, if your firm measures on equity).
  2. Room to the daily floor = today's start minus today's daily floor level.
  3. Room to the total floor = current equity minus the total floor level.
  4. The smaller of the two numbers is today's real room.
  5. Write both numbers down before the first trade, and update the total-floor number at the end of every day.

Which firm's rules count?

Yours. Firms define these limits differently — different percentages, different floor behaviour, balance-based or equity-based measurement. Copy your firm's exact definitions and numbers into your records on day one, and plan from those, not from anyone else's challenge.
