What paperwork should a family caregiver keep in one place?

Ask caregivers what they need and a pattern shows up fast: they are given plenty of advice, empathy and lists — then ask where the actual system is. When a hospital, an insurer or an agency asks a question, the answer is in a drawer, a phone photo, or somebody else's house. The system is one binder where every document, contact and list lives. Here is what goes in it.

The five sections

  • Medical: current medication list with doses, conditions and allergies, doctors and specialists with phone numbers, insurance cards and policy numbers, recent visit summaries.
  • Legal and money: copies of the power of attorney and advance directive, bank and insurance contacts, benefit letters — the papers every office asks for first.
  • Daily care: routines, care notes, what works and what does not — so a covering family member or aide can step in without a briefing.
  • Contacts: family, neighbours, pharmacy, home-care agency, and the after-hours numbers.
  • The paper trail: a simple running log — date, who you spoke to, which office, what they said, what happens next.

Why keep a paper trail?

Caregivers hit dead ends between offices: one agency sends you to another, an application stalls on a missing document, and nobody can say what was already tried. A dated log ends the loop — you can show exactly who said what, when, and which document is still missing. It is the least glamorous page in the binder and often the most useful one.

How do I start?

  1. Start with the medical section — it is the one needed without warning.
  2. Add legal and money copies as you locate them; note where originals are.
  3. Write the contacts page while you have your phone in hand.
  4. Start the paper-trail log with the next call you make, and keep it current.
