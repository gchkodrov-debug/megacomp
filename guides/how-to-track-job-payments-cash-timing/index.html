How do I track payments on every job so cash doesn't run dry between jobs?

The pain, in a contractor's own words, is cash timing: a small shop can be profitable on paper and still break, because money owed sits inside finished jobs while this week's bills land today. The fix is not complicated accounting — it is one money log per job, kept current and reviewed weekly, that shows what each job still owes you, what you still owe out on it, and when the money moves.

What goes in the log for each job?

  • Contract total.
  • Deposit received — amount and date.
  • Progress payments — amount, due date, and the date actually received.
  • Final payment due, and its trigger (completion, sign-off).
  • Money out on the job — materials (date and cost), labour, other costs.
  • Balance still owed to you on the job.
  • Balance you still owe out on the job.

The weekly 15-minute routine

  1. Update every received payment and every cost since last week.
  2. List every payment due to you in the next two weeks, by date.
  3. Chase anything overdue the same day — politely, while it is days overdue, not weeks.
  4. Set the two-week money-in list next to the money going out, before committing to a new job's upfront costs.

The companion habit: a clean handover means the payment schedule on the log matches what was agreed at the sale — see the handover sheet guide.
