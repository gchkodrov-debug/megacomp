What records should I keep in case my prop firm changes the rules or denies my payout?

Traders in prop-firm communities repeat the same story: rules changed overnight, a payout denied over fine print, an account closed after a payout request — and when they argue, they have nothing dated to argue with. If a firm ever moves the goalposts on you, the only version of events that counts is the one you can prove. Keep these records from day one — not from the day something goes wrong.

The evidence folder — what to keep

  • The rules at purchase: the firm's full rule page and challenge terms, saved or screenshotted on the day you buy, with the date visible.
  • The purchase record: confirmation, plan name, account size, price paid.
  • A daily log: date, starting balance, closing balance, and anything unusual that day.
  • Your trade journal: every trade with date, instrument, size and result.
  • Milestone screenshots: the dashboard at each phase change, and again right before any payout request.
  • Payout requests: date, amount, the firm's reply, and the date paid or denied — with the reason they gave, in their words.
  • Support contact: tickets and emails kept whole, with dates — including the ones that went unanswered.
  • Rule-change announcements: saved on the day you see them, not the day they are used against you.

Why does the date on each record matter?

Because a rule page saved after a dispute proves nothing about what the rule was when you traded. Dated records show two things at once: what you agreed to on the day you bought, and what your account actually did, day by day. That is the whole case — in either direction.

How often do I update it?

Daily balances take under a minute at the end of each trading day. The rest is event-based: save things when they happen — a purchase, a phase change, a payout request, an announcement. The folder only works if it is boring and complete.
