What details does my crew need before they start a job?

A job can be sold perfectly and still fall apart before it starts — in the handover. The crew arrives on site missing half the details that were agreed during the sale: what is included, who supplies the materials, who can approve a change. The fix is one handover sheet, filled in at the moment of the sale, that carries the whole job from the office to the site.

The 12 details on the handover sheet

  1. Client name and best contact number.
  2. Site address, access and parking.
  3. The scope in writing — what is included, and what is not.
  4. Materials list, and who supplies each item.
  5. Measurements, drawings and site photos.
  6. Start date, daily working hours and the deadline.
  7. Who on site can approve changes.
  8. Agreed price and the payment schedule.
  9. Permits or inspections the job needs.
  10. Site conditions and hazards.
  11. Cleanup and disposal — whose job it is.
  12. How completion gets signed off.

When do I fill it in?

At the sale, not the morning of the job. The person who sold the job has every one of these details in their head or in the estimate; the sheet is how they leave the head and reach the hands. If a detail is genuinely unknown, write “TBC” on the sheet — a visible gap gets closed, an unspoken one becomes a phone call from site.
