The Wall, Approached

Scroll. The camera flies the path the rig was given — no cuts, because the path is one curve. The wall has forty-seven bricks: the Golden Wall's own number.

The journey, as text (the reduced-motion presentation)

  1. The approach. From the street, the town is a wall and a coin.
  2. The coin. The camera closes and swings around it; it turns as you descend.
  3. The lighting. Course by course, the wall's bricks light in order.
  4. The whole. Forty-seven bricks lit; the camera rises and withdraws.

How the flight works

The director's instructions are five keyframes — a scroll position, a camera position, and a point to look at. Between them the rig flies a Catmull-Rom curve: it passes through every keyframe with the same tangent leaving as arriving, so there is no corner to catch on and no cut to hide. Scroll position is the only input; the pose is a pure function of it, which is why scrolling back up flies the same path home. Continuity here is by construction, and the exam harness measures it anyway: forty landings down the page, and no step may jump.

The objects take their cues from the same scroll: the coin's turning is a function of the scroll position, and the bricks light in a fixed order across the third leg of the flight — camera, coin, and wall reading one clock.

Study build: Track 7's M11 exam artifact, served locally for grading — the scroll-driven upgrade pattern for a certain coin page, demonstrated here and deployed nowhere. The coin is the same procedural model as Project 5's; the wall is forty-seven boxes and one idea.
