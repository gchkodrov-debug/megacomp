# PRESENTATION STANDARD — RECONCILIATION AMENDMENT: ONE FILM PER PRODUCT

**Filed:** 2026-11-23 · **Status:** AMENDMENT PAPER — the captain files this with the Presentation Standard (2026-11-22). **This paper does not edit the Presentation Standard itself.** When the captain files it, the replacement sentences below are the governing text wherever they apply; the original passages remain in place as the historical record until the standard's own text is next revised under the founder's Q14 ruling ("reconciled in the Presentation Standard's own text before implementation" — implementation has not been authorised; nothing here authorises it).

**The ruling reconciled.** Founder's Q14 (2026-11-23; decisions log, Q14 line): *"ONE film per product, not two; the duration requirement is reconciled inside the Presentation Standard's own text before implementation."* This settles flag F-9 / register R-14 of the Mint Listing Standard, which had flagged the conflict without resolving it: the Presentation Standard's frame math runs from an **8-second** film, while the standing media law in the same document calls the scroll film **10–15 s** — and the record nowhere said whether those were one asset or two. **They are one asset. There is one film.**

---

## §1 — THE SINGLE GOVERNING SPEC (one sentence)

> **Each product has exactly ONE scroll film: a single purpose-made film of the product's own assembly — whole, turning, coming apart layer by layer, reassembling — from which the scroll sequence is sliced at up to 30 fps into ~240 frames (never under 200); the frame count governs, and the film lasts as long as the product's assembly honestly takes at the standard's slow-motion pace — at a full 240-frame, 30 fps slice, about eight seconds of source film.**

## §2 — WHY THIS SENTENCE IS THE RECONCILIATION (the evidence)

1. **The frame math is exact, and it was always the binding number.** 240 frames ÷ 30 fps = 8 seconds. The "8-second film" of Craft Rule 1 is not a second, competing duration — it is the arithmetic of the frame bar itself (fps × seconds = frames, as the rule states). The Mint Listing Standard §3.2 had already recorded that, pending reconciliation, "the binding constraints… are the frame count, the budgets, and the CWV bars, which both readings must satisfy."
2. **The founder's slow-motion correction removed seconds as the governing unit.** The journey plays in slow motion: *"smooth, unhurried, however long it takes; seconds do not matter, the assembly must be seen"* (the founder's standing correction, as recorded at Listing Standard §3.1). A spec whose governing unit is seconds contradicts that correction; a spec governed by frame count does not.
3. **The 10–15 s figure cannot govern the same asset.** Sliced at 30 fps, a 10–15 s film yields 300–450 frames — outside the ~240-frame bar on its face. It also breaks the budgets at the specimen's measured frame sizes (Listing Standard, Annex A: the specimen's 235 frames measure 6,302,660 B desktop and 1,486,596 B mobile — ≈26.8 KB and ≈6.3 KB per frame; at those sizes 300 frames ≈ 1.9 MB mobile against the ≤1.5 MB bar, and 450 frames ≈ 12.1 MB desktop against the ≤8.5 MB bar). The record contains no arithmetic check of the media-law line against Craft Rule 1 — the line was carried as flagged-and-unresolved (F-9 / R-14) until the founder ruled — and the builder's reading, offered as a reading and not as drafting history, is that the line described generous real assembly motion in a list written the same day the bar was set. As a governing duration for the scroll film it is superseded by §1 above.
4. **The specimen proves the reconciled shape.** The Mint specimen — the standard's own named first test of the bar (Presentation Standard, "First test of the bar": Commission 17, the Coin Mint service) — carries **235 frames** and passed its final grading **6/6**, with both sequence budgets measured and CWV filed on the grading record (Listing Standard, Annex A). One film, frame-governed: that is the shape that has actually been built and graded.
5. **The 10–15 s figure survives where it belongs.** The media law separately specifies the **UGC-style hook video at 10–15 s**. The hook video is a different asset (it earns attention in the first seconds; it is not sliced into the scroll sequence) and is **not** touched by this reconciliation. Only the scroll-film line is superseded.
