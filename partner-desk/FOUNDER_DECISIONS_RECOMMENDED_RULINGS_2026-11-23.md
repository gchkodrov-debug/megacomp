### R-1 — Legal, technical, and financial clearance of the payment arrangements
**The question, in plain words:** Are the payment arrangements — converting proceeds into PROVADIA, running the 80/20 split, and paying the founder in fiat — verified as legal, technical, and financial, so the till can be treated as real?
**Recommended ruling:** No clearance is recommended as done — nothing is cleared. The recommendation is a clearance PATH: all three verifications the record names must be completed — legal (with house counsel), technical, and financial. The record does not order them; the crew's suggested order is legal first (its answer can stop the other two being wasted work), then technical, then financial — a proposal, not a record rule. Until all three are verified, the till stays unwired, the buy area stays disabled, and the honest status block stays printed.
**Why (house-record basis):** THE WHOLE HOUSE §6 says the arrangements "still need legal, technical, and financial verification before anyone treats them as operational"; Register R-1 makes this the block on till wiring (sequence step 2), and Mint Standard §5.3 places the end-to-end journey verification there.
**What it unblocks:** Till wiring (sequence step 2) — but only after each verification is done.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** The house would treat rails as operational before verifying them, printing a safety it does not have.

### R-2 — Charges-live conditions
**The question, in plain words:** Are the two go-live conditions — a dated public notice changing the word "free" and the rehearsal validation — each done, and what is the notice's date and text?
**Recommended ruling:** Neither condition is confirmed in the record. The founder should treat charges as blocked until he fixes and documents the notice's date and text, confirms the supersession of every printed "free" wording, and confirms the rehearsal validation per the Answer 12 guards.
**Why (house-record basis):** F-1 says whether the two conditions are satisfied is NOT in the record; Mint Standard §5.3 makes both conditions prerequisites for any charge.
**What it unblocks:** Any charge, on any listing.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** Charging while the printed word "free" still appears anywhere — the house would take money under one word and print another.

### R-3 — Rails confirmation
**The question, in plain words:** Should the till be wired to three rails (card, PayPal, crypto) or PROVADIA-only?
**Recommended ruling:** The founder's explicit word should confirm three rails — card, PayPal, crypto — per THE WHOLE HOUSE §6, with the 20% PROVADIA discount, not PROVADIA-only.
**Why (house-record basis):** THE WHOLE HOUSE §6 is the governing document and designs three rails; the standard's operating text follows it and flags F-2 for the founder's confirmation.
**What it unblocks:** The till's shape when wired.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** PROVADIA-only would make buying PROVADIA a de facto requirement for every service — colliding with F-4 and R-4 — and contradict the governing document.

### R-4 — The purchase-required fork
**The question, in plain words:** Does anyone ever have to buy or hold PROVADIA to use the Mint service, or any service?
**Recommended ruling:** No purchase or holding of PROVADIA is ever a requirement of the Mint service. A signature qualifies, not a purchase. PROVADIA is only an optional payment method at a 20% discount.
**Why (house-record basis):** Mint Standard §§1.7 and 5.2 — the standard's *operating text* — state that no purchase or holding is required, and §1.7 states holding is never an admission test, a priority, or a condition of the service. Flag F-4 is explicit that the underlying specification does **not** settle this fork, so this recommendation asks the founder's word to settle it in line with the operating text — it does not enforce a settled law, because on this point there is none yet.
**What it unblocks:** Removes any implied PROVADIA purchase obligation from every listing.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** The Mint becomes a coin-selling funnel rather than a service, and an unruled founder remark (F-4) overrides the standard's operating text without ever being decided.

### R-5 — Delivery duration
**The question, in plain words:** Is there any fixed per-launch delivery timeframe beyond the queue law?
**Recommended ruling:** No fixed per-launch timeframe is set. Listings state queue facts only — his number, who is ahead, the desk's measured pace — plus the 72-hour call-response rule and the caps. No dates.
**Why (house-record basis):** Settled law forbids promised birth dates; Mint Standard §6.4 allows as stated facts the queue shape, the 72-hour response window, and the caps — and no duration.
**What it unblocks:** Listings can print queue facts without inventing a duration.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** A fixed duration becomes a promised birth date, breaking settled law.

### R-6 — PROVADIA price mechanics
**The question, in plain words:** When a buyer pays in PROVADIA, how does the $50 or $40 dollar price turn into a PROVADIA amount — what rate, when is it fixed, and who eats the movement?
**Recommended ruling:** NO HOUSE BASIS — founder's free choice. Option A: fix the PROVADIA amount at checkout using a disclosed rate source; the buyer bears movement between quote and payment. Option B: fix it at receipt by the house's daily batch rate; the house bears movement.
**Why (house-record basis):** THE WHOLE HOUSE §6 covers the house's conversion of proceeds into PROVADIA (at receipt or in a disclosed daily batch), not the buyer-facing price conversion. The record gives no rate source or moment of fixing for the buyer's PROVADIA amount.
**What it unblocks:** A clear buyer-facing PROVADIA price when the till goes live.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** Ambiguity on who bears movement — the house could end up eating a rate swing, or the buyer could be surprised at checkout.

### R-7 — Refund mechanics for charged services
**The question, in plain words:** If someone pays the fee and then declines, withdraws, or the town STOPs the build — what comes back, in what asset, on what timeline?
**Recommended ruling:** NO HOUSE BASIS for the mechanics themselves — founder's free choice. The settled guard stands either way: chain costs are never refunded by the town (they were never the town's money), and until R-7 is set, no listing may print or imply a refund promise for the fee.
**Why (house-record basis):** Mint Standard §6.5 says what happens to the fee on decline-after-payment, withdrawal, or STOPPED is NOT settled (R-7); F-7 ties R-7's content to R-12.
**What it unblocks:** Honest refund wording once the founder sets the mechanics.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** Printing a refund promise for an unsettled fee would break the honesty sentence and §6.5's guard.

### R-13 — The split's base under the discount
**The question, in plain words:** When a buyer pays the discounted $40, does the 80/20 split run on $40 ($32 founder / $8 pot) or is the pot's share fixed at $10 ($30 founder / $10 pot)?
**Recommended ruling:** NO HOUSE BASIS — founder's free choice. Option A: the split runs on the amount paid ($32/$8). Option B: the pot's share is fixed at $10 ($30/$10). Until ruled, the standard's interim guard holds: beside a discounted price, print percentages only, never a dollar split.
**Why (house-record basis):** F-8: §4.2's $40/$10 is the split OF $50; §4.3's $40 is the discounted price; the record gives both rules and never their combination.
**What it unblocks:** A clear split when a discounted payment arrives.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** Mixing the two $40s would misstate the pot's share — the exact confusion flag F-8 exists to prevent.

### R-14 — The film-duration reconciliation
**The question, in plain words:** The Presentation Standard's math runs from an ~8-second film, but the media law calls the scroll film 10–15 seconds. One asset or two?
**Recommended ruling:** NO HOUSE BASIS — founder's free choice. Option A: one asset — the scroll film is the ~8-second film, and the 10–15 s wording is read as a maximum or older text, reconciled in the Presentation Standard. Option B: two assets — an ~8-second presentation film and a separate 10–15 s scroll film for media.
**Why (house-record basis):** F-9: one asset or two is not stated in the record; binding constraints either way are frame count ≥200, desktop sequence ≤8.5 MB, mobile ≤1.5 MB, and the CWV bars.
**What it unblocks:** A single, consistent film specification for the Presentation Standard.
**Risk if ruled the other way:** Leaving the conflict unresolved means two different durations in the house's own text, with the size and performance bars applied inconsistently.
