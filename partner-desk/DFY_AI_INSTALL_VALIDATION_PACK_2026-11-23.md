**What gets installed** (house design, grounded in the analogues' documented scopes):
- A small fleet of working agents inside the buyer's own tools — drawn from: a **support agent** (answers customer questions from the buyer's policies/FAQs, checks order/status info, hands off with context), a **follow-up agent** (DM/email follow-ups, review requests, abandoned conversations), a **scheduling/admin agent** (booking, reminders, intake sorting). Tier decides how many.
- Each agent trained on the **buyer's own documents** — his policies, price lists, FAQs, past answers — not generic templates.
- The **#511 command records installed as the control layer**: the roster of what each agent may and may not do, per-agent cost caps, the QA scorecard, and the kill switch / keep-kill review — the thing neither analogue sells as the client's permanent property.
- **Guardrails set with the buyer**: approved actions, money limits (e.g. refund ceiling), escalation rules — the pattern Adelante publishes (buyer-set rules, limits, and escalations) and sells with.

**For whom:** a small business owner (roughly 2–50 staff) who already fields repetitive customer messages, follow-ups, or bookings by hand — e-commerce stores, service businesses, clinics, agencies — and who has tried chatbots himself or means to "get AI set up" but has no one to do it. The Census data in §2 says this is the majority of the market: the smallest firms are the slowest to have started.

**In what time:** a **10-business-day install window** is the design target — Adelante documents 10 days for a single support agent; Agentibus promises first agent live within 30 days. Ten days for a small fleet is ambitious but not invented; it is a *target the validation pilot must prove*, not a market fact.

**What the buyer can do the day after that he could not do before:**
- His repetitive customer questions are answered in his voice, from his policies, at hours he is not working — and anything sensitive arrives in his inbox **with the full story attached**, not as a bare escalation.
- Follow-ups he used to forget (quotes chased, reviews requested, no-shows re-booked) happen on schedule without him touching them.
- He can **see and stop his fleet**: one roster showing every agent, what it did, what it cost him in usage, and an off-switch per agent. Before: a vague intention to "use AI someday." After: a working crew with a leash, in his own accounts, that he owns outright.

## 3. DELIVERY ECONOMICS

One install, costed honestly. **Stated assumptions** (each is a choice, named so the founder can strike or change it):

- **A1 — Labor is crew + captain time, supervised by the founder on client-facing calls.** The crew's marginal cash cost is near zero (already-running AI labor); the founder's hours are the scarce currency. Both are costed below at **shadow rates** — because a business that only works when the founder's time is priced at $0 does not work.
- **A2 — The fleet runs on the BUYER's accounts and keys** (the #511 doctrine: per-agent cost caps on the buyer's own usage). AI usage/inference costs are the buyer's, metered to him — if we ever pay the usage, the margin math below dies; see the loss point.
- **A3 — Tooling we consume during delivery** (our model bench, connectors, testing) is estimated at **$10–$40 per install** cash ($25 mid used below).
- **A4 — Payment cost:** Stripe at ~2.9% + $0.30 per charge (standard published rate; the house's own processing is via Shopify/Stripe on the store's rails). **Processing fees are NOT returned on refunds** — this matters in §4 and §5.
- **A5 — Support window: 30 days post-handover**, per the founder's Mint rulings pattern (30-day full support, no response-time promise). Market norm check: Adelante bundles weekly tuning into its subscription and a 30-day money-back guarantee; a bare one-time install with NO support window would be below the market's shape.

### 3.1 The work in one install (Fleet tier, 3 agents — the middle case)

| Task | Hours (crew + founder) |
|---|---|
| Discovery & workflow mapping (buyer call, process read, doc collection) | 2–3 |
| Buyer-doc preparation (policies/FAQs/past answers cleaned into training material) | 2–4 |
| Build & configure per agent (prompts, guardrails, integrations, knowledge) × 3 | 9–15 (3–5 each) |
| Testing on the buyer's real cases + fix rounds | 3–5 |
| Records install (#511 roster, cost caps, QA scorecard, kill switch) — templated, it exists | 1–2 |
| Handover session + buyer training | 1–2 |
| **Install subtotal** | **18–31 hrs** |
| 30-day support burden (tuning, "it answered wrong" incidents, one fix round) | 3–6 |
| **Total per install** | **≈21–37 hrs** |

The Starter tier (1 agent) compresses to **≈9–14 hrs total**; Command adds the training session and a scheduled tuning pass, **≈26–44 hrs**. These are estimates from the analogues' documented scopes (Adelante's 10-day single-agent launch; Agentibus's ≤30-day first agent) — **no house install has ever been delivered, so every hour figure here is UNPROVEN until a pilot measures it.**

### 3.2 Margin at the three price points

Cash view first (what actually leaves the bank): price − Stripe fee − tooling ($25 mid) = **cash contribution ≈ $455 / $747 / $941** at $495/$795/$995. On a pure cash basis every tier "works" — and that statement is nearly meaningless, because the real cost is hours.

Shadow view — gross margin after labor costed at two shadow rates (mid-case hours: Starter 11.5 · Fleet 29 · Command 35). *Tooling (~$25/install) is excluded from this table; including it lowers every margin by $25 and every break-even by ~0.5 hr:*

| Tier | Price | Net of Stripe | Margin @ $25/hr shadow | Margin @ $50/hr shadow | **Break-even hours @ $50/hr** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter $495 | $495 | ~$480 | $480 − $288 = **+$192 (40%)** | $480 − $575 = **−$95 (LOSES)** | ~9.6 hrs |
| Fleet $795 | $795 | ~$772 | $772 − $725 = **+$47 (6%)** | $772 − $1,450 = **−$678 (LOSES)** | ~15.4 hrs |
| Command $995 | $995 | ~$966 | $966 − $875 = **+$91 (9%)** | $966 − $1,750 = **−$784 (LOSES)** | ~19.3 hrs |

### 3.3 The verdict, plainly

- **The loss point exists and it is hours.** At any labor value near market ($50/hr for competent implementation work), **all three tiers lose money at the estimated hours**. The offer only clears at $50/hr if delivery is radically templated: Starter ≤9 hrs, Fleet ≤15 hrs, Command ≤19 hrs — roughly **half** the mid-case estimates.
- **$495 is a door-opener, not a business**: it survives only as a single-agent, template-driven install with a hard scope fence. The first messy buyer (missing policies, three fix rounds) puts it underwater at any honest rate.
- **$795 is the honest floor** for the 3-agent fleet, and only with the #511 records pre-templated (they are) and a contractual scope fence (agent count, channel count, one fix round).
- **$995 is the first tier with real air** — ~19 break-even hours at $50/hr — provided the Command scope does not quietly grow a second training session or open-ended tuning.
- **The structural disadvantage, named:** Adelante charges **$0** for this install because its run (published entry $900/mo) pays for it over time. Our house rule keeps the run custom-quote-only, so our install fee must carry the full delivery cost alone, against competitors whose install is free. The install fee is therefore not really competing with Adelante's install price (zero) — it competes with the buyer's *total* commitment: one payment and he owns it, vs a monthly fee that never ends. That ownership pitch is the offer's actual edge, and the validation test must find out if buyers feel it.
- **Capacity ceiling:** at ~21–37 hours per Fleet install and a founder who must front every client relationship, the honest ceiling is **2–3 installs/month** without quality or the rest of the house breaking — matching the Pipeline's "seat-capped slots" flag. This is a boutique, not a conveyor, at these prices.
- **The No-Loss Rule check** (founder's rule, 2026-11-23 — the house never loses money on a transaction): under the No-Loss mechanics (costs off the top, split on net), an install whose hours overrun is a loss the split cannot fix. The rule therefore requires the scope fence and the hour caps above to be *contract terms*, not hopes — and a pilot that measures real hours before any price is called final.

## 4. THE VALIDATION PLAN — A PROPOSAL FOR THE FOUNDER (nothing here is executed)

**Design principle:** the cheapest honest test that can *kill* this line, in the house test style — numbers, a window, a death condition — run before any build beyond the test page itself. The evidence gap (§2) is specific: *will a small business pay a one-time $495–$995 to have an agent fleet installed, when the market's proven shape charges $0 install + a monthly run fee?* The test must ask exactly that, with money, not opinions.

**Approval discipline:** approval of this pack is NOT approval of any step. Each step below begins only on the founder's separate word for that step. Nothing in this section is authorized by the pack's existence.

### Step 1 — The offer page + deposit door (the demand test)

- **What:** ONE offer page on onejobkits.com for the **#511 AI Agent Fleet DFY Install** — the three tiers as in §1.2, the ownership pitch ("installed in your accounts, you own it, no monthly fee"), the slot cap stated as the honest ceiling ("2–3 install slots per month," §3.3), and two doors: (a) a **waitlist** opt-in, (b) a **$99 deposit pre-order** — refundable, credited in full against any tier, taken through the store's existing live Stripe/Shopify rails.
- **Window:** 45 days from the page going live.
- **The bars, evaluated in this order (PASS first, then EXTEND, then KILL — a result can only land in one):**
  1. **PASS:** **≥10 deposit-paid pre-orders** at $99 — deposits, not clicks — **or ≥3 buyers paying a full tier in advance** for a pilot slot. Pass → the founder rules on a capped pilot (Step 3).
  2. **EXTEND:** PASS not met, but ≥40 waitlist opt-ins AND ≥3 deposits → ONE 30-day extension with a changed angle or tier emphasis, founder's call. No second extension.
  3. **KILL:** PASS not met AND (opt-ins <40 **or** deposits <3) at day 45 → **the line dies.** Deposits refunded, the page comes down, #511 remains a boxed product, and the result is reported like every house test verdict. (Mirrors the SB 553 bar's shape: 300 downloads / 40 opt-ins / 10 pre-orders or it dies, by Dec 17, 2026.)
- **The test's own cost, named — including its No-Loss exception:** building the page on existing rails costs crew time only. The money cost is refunds: **Stripe keeps its processing fee on every refunded charge — ~$3.17 per $99 deposit, ~$29.16 per $995 prepay.** Worst case at the KILL bar (39 deposits refunded) ≈ **$124**; a killed pilot with three $995 prepays refunded ≈ **$87** in kept fees. That is a real, bounded loss the founder's No-Loss Rule does not permit by default — so it is stated here as **the test's price**: the founder accepts this capped exposure explicitly when he approves the deposit door, or the door is structured differently (e.g. card authorization without capture) on his ruling. The test is **not** "near zero cost" and this pack does not call it that.
- **Crew can do alone (after the founder's Step 1 word):** build the page from the existing #511 assets, wire the deposit SKU on existing rails, instrument counts, keep the evidence log, report weekly.
- **Needs the founder first:** approve the page copy and the three prices as *test* prices · **rule the #511 price relationship (§1.2 flag)** before copy is written · approve taking refundable deposits at all, including the capped fee exposure above · approve any promotion of the page beyond the store's own surfaces (his standing organic channels).

### Step 2 — The conversation leg (run in parallel, because §2.2 signal 5 says this market sells in conversations)

- **What:** 10 structured problem interviews with small-business owners from audiences the house already lawfully holds (store list, Discord, community rooms) — 30 minutes each: their repetitive-work pain, what they've tried, what they'd pay to have it *installed and owned* vs rented monthly at the published entry shape ($0 install + $900/mo). No pitch until the last five minutes; the install offer is described and the deposit door named.
- **Bar:** **≥4 of 10** say, unprompted on price, that one-time-and-owned beats the rental shape for them, **and ≥2 of the 10 convert to deposits.** Below that, the page's numbers are read as curiosity, not demand.
- **Known weakness, stated in advance:** owners drawn from the house's own audiences are predisposed to be kind. The interview bar can be passed on friendliness alone — which is why **Step 1's deposit bar governs** and this leg only explains or discredits it.
- **Crew can do alone:** draft the interview script and the evidence sheet.
- **Needs the founder:** his word before ANY outreach happens (no outreach is authorized by this pack) · he fronts or co-fronts the calls — in this market the founder IS the trust.

### Step 3 — The paid pilot (only if Step 1 passes; founder-ruled)

- **What:** deliver **2–3 installs maximum** at the paid tier, time-tracked to the hour, scope-fenced in writing (agent count, channels, one fix round, 30-day support), with the handover test the Mint standard uses: the buyer operates his fleet unaided.
- **Pilot PASS bar:** median delivery **≤15 hrs (Fleet)** with the buyer passing the unaided-operation handover, **and** a written testimonial-grade satisfaction answer, **and** zero refunds. Pass → the founder rules on opening the line at capped slots.
- **Pilot KILL bar:** median hours >19 on Fleet (the $50/hr break-even), OR any refund for non-delivery, OR a buyer-facing agent error that reaches a customer uncaught → the line returns to the founder with a kill recommendation.

**Total founder cost of the full test:** page/copy approval and the #511 ruling (~1 hr) · the deposit ruling · up to 10 half-hour calls if he fronts them · the pilot ruling · the capped refund-fee exposure named in Step 1. **What the test never does:** build the service beyond the test page, hire anyone, or promise a slot the capacity ceiling (§3.3) cannot serve.

## 5. RISKS + KILL CONDITIONS

1. **The deliverability gap (the partner's own caveat, adopted).** A competitor's verified revenue proves a *market exists*; it does not prove OneJobKits can deliver the same service profitably. Our delivery hours are unmeasured, our install has never been performed, and the two true analogues are subscription businesses whose economics we are deliberately not copying. This is the pack's central risk and the reason §4 is a test, not a launch.
2. **Platform dependency — two kinds.** (a) *Rule risk:* the fleet lives inside other companies' systems — model APIs (pricing and behavior change without notice), Meta/WhatsApp messaging rules, Zendesk/Gorgias/Shopify app policies. Any one of them can raise the delivery cost, degrade an agent, or ban a pattern mid-flight. Buyer-owned accounts spread the account risk to the buyer, but the *reputation* risk of a broken fleet lands on us. (b) *Substitution risk — the more plausible killer:* the platforms themselves are shipping native AI agents (helpdesk vendors bundle their own agents; Shopify bundles AI into the store; model vendors sell small-business agents directly). If the buyer's existing tools hand him a "good enough" agent free or bundled, the paid-install market can shrink to the complex cases only. Adelante itself lives inside Zendesk's marketplace while Zendesk builds native AI — the analogue carries this exposure too; that it still sells is evidence, not immunity.
3. **Credential custody and customer-data exposure.** Delivery requires the crew to hold **admin-level access to the buyer's systems** and routes the buyer's **customer conversation and order data through model APIs**. No custody protocol, least-privilege access pattern, or data-handling terms exist in the house today. A buyer's customer-data incident during our install window is our reputational event and possibly a legal one. **No pilot may run before a written custody/data protocol exists** — building that protocol is part of Step 3's preparation, and it is a founder-approved document, not a crew habit.
4. **Delivery-capacity ceiling.** Founder-fronted trust + ~21–37 crew hours per install = 2–3 installs/month honestly. Success beyond that ceiling is itself a failure mode (a queue we cannot serve, refunds, reputation). The seat-cap is not marketing; it is the business's real shape at these prices.
5. **Refund exposure.** The market's norm is a 30-day money-back guarantee (Adelante). If we match it, one refunded $995 install sinks ~35 hours and returns $0 (minus the ~$29 Stripe keeps). The arithmetic at shadow rates: two refunds among the first five Command installs return ~$1,990 of revenue while their ~70 delivery hours are already spent — against the three surviving installs' combined shadow margin of ~$273 at $25/hr, **two refunds erase the line's contribution entirely** (at pure cash view they do not — the loss is in hours, which is the house's scarce currency). If we *don't* match the guarantee, we sell a bare offer against a guarantee-carrying competitor. Either way it must be a founder ruling, priced into the test.
6. **Buyer-facing error risk.** An installed agent will eventually answer a customer wrongly — a wrong refund, a wrong promise. Guardrails and escalation rules reduce it; they do not remove it. One screenshot of a bad answer can cost more than one install earns.
7. **Evidence decay.** The category moves monthly: the set's #3 receipt (White Glove) fell across the quarter and has been listed for sale since September. Any figure in §2 is a snapshot; a validation window that drags past ~90 days is testing against a market that may have moved.
8. **The one-time-fee structural risk (§3.3).** If buyers simply prefer $0-down-and-monthly — the shape both analogues prove — the install fee may be unsellable at any of the three prices, and the honest fallback is the convergence's own second door: a run-plan by custom quote. That fallback is a *founder* decision, not a crew pivot.
