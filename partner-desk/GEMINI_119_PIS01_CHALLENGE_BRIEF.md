## The standard in one paragraph
PIS-01 grades a living universe on 15 disciplines totaling 100 points (dimensional transportation 10 · cinematic composition 10 · environmental depth 8 · environmental life 8 · emotional impact 10 · physical consistency 7 · environmental sound 5 · lighting 5 · Solnik integration 8 · interactivity 7 · universe individuality 5 · portal continuity 4 · performance 5 · commercial clarity 5 · visitor comfort 3), with technical requirements (desktop 60 FPS · mobile stable 30 FPS min · load ≤2.5 s · interaction ≤200 ms · visual feedback <100 ms · CLS ≤0.01 internal target · zero visible loop interruptions · zero broken interactions · zero character clipping · mobile initial transfer ≤1.5 MB · 30-minute stability/thermal test), a five-level ladder (BASIC → MEDIUM → HIGH-END → PLATINUM → PLATINUM-PRISTINE), Platinum-Pristine at ≥95/100 + zero critical defects + independent human validation, and a human examination: ≥20 unfamiliar visitors, unprimed, rating 10 questions 1–10. Its science rests on Mel Slater's presence research (Place Illusion · Plausibility Illusion · sensorimotor contingency).

## Challenge — answer with evidence, not agreement
1. **Science:** Is Slater's framework correctly applied to a 2D browser window (no HMD)? What does the presence literature actually support for flat-screen "transportation," and what would a defensible measurement instrument be (established presence questionnaires vs our custom 10)? Where is the directive overclaiming?
2. **Thresholds:** Interrogate every number. Is CLS ≤0.01 achievable and meaningful for a layered cinematic page, or is it noise below measurement reliability? Is "zero visible loop interruptions" the right formulation vs a measured seam metric (the house loop gate is RMSE ≤8)? Is 30 FPS mobile "stable" defined (frame-time variance? p95)? Is ≤1.5 MB mobile initial transfer compatible with a cinematic universe — and what should count toward it?
3. **Feasibility:** On real phones (mobile Safari WebGL limits, thermal throttling over 30 minutes), which requirements are at risk first? What degrades gracefully and what breaks the illusion outright?
4. **Methodology:** The 20-visitor examination — sample, priming controls, question wording (several questions are leading), score aggregation (how do 10 question-means become discipline points?), and the independence problem: the founder judges his own build. Design the smallest examination whose result you would trust.
5. **The ladder:** Are the five levels discriminating (could two very different builds land on the same level)? Is 95/100 the right Platinum-Pristine bar, and are the discipline weights sane (e.g., visitor comfort at 3 vs transportation at 10)?
