# REFUND POLICY — THE TOWN'S SERVICES (DRAFT)

**DRAFT — NOT IN FORCE.** This policy takes effect only when (a) the founder approves its final text, and (b) charging is otherwise cleared under the Q1 clearance checks and the Q2 charges-live conditions. Until then it is a paper draft: it creates no rights, makes no promises, and may not be printed on any listing. Filed 2026-11-23, as the founder's Q7 ruling ordered (a clear refund policy must EXIST before any money is accepted).

**Scope.** The Coin Mint service first. The same policy is the template for the town's other services, downloadable products, and higher-ticket work, varied only as §9 states (per the Mint Listing Standard §8). Where a written quote for higher-ticket work sets its own refund terms, those terms govern that work and this policy is the floor beneath them.

**The shape of the money (why this policy is short).** Under the founder's rulings, the service fee is charged **at completion only** (Q12) — completion being the handover test: the buyer can operate his coin unaided and the record is written (Listing Standard §6.3). The town therefore holds no service fee during the work, and most situations that fill other houses' refund policies cannot arise here: there is nothing to give back, because nothing was taken. The cases that remain are covered below, each with what is returned, in what asset, and on what timeline. Timelines in this draft are **proposed terms for the founder's approval**, stated in calendar days from the Day-Book receipt that opens the case.

---

## §1 — THE TWO FLOWS, NEVER MERGED

1.1 **The chain's own creation costs** are paid by the buyer from his own wallet directly to the chain at launch. The town never receives, fronts, holds, or co-signs them. **The town cannot refund what it never held**, and no wording in this policy may be read otherwise. What the chain does with its own costs is the chain's affair. [CLEARANCE-CHECK: whether any consumer-protection rule could reach the town for costs it never held is a legal question for the Q1 clearance; this draft states none.]

1.2 **The service fee** moves once, at completion, through the till, on one of the three ruled rails (card · PayPal · crypto). Only money in this flow can ever be the subject of a return under this policy.

1.3 **The No-Loss Rule binds every return** — the house never ends a transaction in the red — **subject to one bounded exception, proposed at §5.3, which is not part of this policy unless the founder approves it expressly as an amendment to his own No-Loss Rule.** Returns restore what was received; they are not a source of buyer profit (no return ever exceeds the amount received). Provider and network costs of the original transaction are covered by the transaction itself, per the founder's amendment to Q13; the split (§8) runs on net receipts only. Where a return and the costs already consumed by the original transaction cannot both be honoured without putting the house in the red, §5.3 and the §8.2 waterfall say exactly who bears what — nothing is left to implication.

## §2 — CUSTOMER CANCELLATION (WITHDRAWN)

2.1 **Before completion** — the buyer may step back at any time, for any reason, without giving one. The ticket is receipted WITHDRAWN. **No fee has been charged, so nothing is returned.** Any place he held in the queue ends with the withdrawal; re-application stands open at the back of the line, as the specification provides, when the intake gate is open.

2.2 **At or after completion** — the service has been delivered and the fee charged. A change of mind after the handover test is met is not a refund case under this policy; the included 30-day full-support window (Listing Standard §7.1) is the road for anything that is wrong, and correction inside that window is made in the open. [CLEARANCE-CHECK: whether applicable consumer law gives a buyer any post-delivery cancellation or cooling-off right for a service of this kind, in the jurisdictions the town sells into — to be established by the Q1 legal clearance; if such a right exists, it applies in full and this section yields to it.]

## §3 — TOWN REFUSAL

3.1 **Refusal before any charge** (the ordinary case). The scope checks (Q11: feasibility · legality · custody · founder readiness · prohibited activities) run before work, and the fee runs only at completion, so a refusal normally precedes any payment. **No fee has been charged, so nothing is returned.** Every refusal is explained to the applicant in writing and receipted in the record, with the application keeping its number (Q11; Listing Standard §6.5).

3.2 **A refusal where money was somehow taken first** can arise only through error (see §5) or a future change to the fee's due-moment, which the founder has not ruled. If the town holds any part of the fee for an application it refuses, that amount is returned **in full, in the same asset it was paid in**, within **[14] days** of the receipted refusal — funded under the §8.2 waterfall and subject to §5.3 where third-party costs of the original transaction were already consumed.

## §4 — FAILED DELIVERY / TOWN STOPPED BUILDS

4.1 **STOPPED** — the town starts a build and does not finish it — is receipted as the town's act, never dressed as buyer silence (Mint Service Specification; Listing Standard §6.5). Because the fee is charged at completion only, a stopped build has normally **taken no fee, and nothing is returned**; the buyer's queue position and priority are handled under the ticket rules (if the town misses its own slot, the founder keeps his priority).

4.2 If any fee amount was in fact received toward a build the town then stops (error, or a future due-moment change), it is returned **in full, in the same asset it was paid in**, within **[14] days** of the receipted STOPPED state — funded under the §8.2 waterfall and subject to §5.3 where third-party costs of the original transaction were already consumed.

4.3 Chain costs the buyer paid to the chain before a stop are governed by §1.1: they were never the town's, and the town does not return them. The rehearsal-before-irreversibility step exists so that a stop, when it comes, ordinarily comes before any chain cost is paid; where the town's own guidance led the buyer to pay a chain cost for a build the town then stops, that fact is receipted in the open with the stop. [CLEARANCE-CHECK: whether any legal duty could attach to the town in that circumstance is for the Q1 clearance; this draft promises nothing about it either way.]

## §5 — MISTAKEN CHARGES

5.1 A mistaken charge is: a duplicate charge · a charge for the wrong amount · a charge made after a withdrawal or refusal · a charge where completion was never met · any charge the till made in error. The buyer need only point at it; the Day-Book and the rail's own record decide what happened.

5.2 A mistaken charge is returned **in full — the whole amount taken — in the same asset and by the same rail it arrived on** (card → the same card through the provider; PayPal → the same PayPal account; PROVADIA → the paying wallet; SOL → the paying wallet — SOL and PROVADIA are the two crypto fee assets of the ruled till: Listing Standard §5.1, §4.3), within **[7] days** of the mistake being receipted, funded under the §8.2 waterfall and subject to §5.3's allocation of any third-party costs the original transaction already consumed. Where the buyer paid in PROVADIA under a fixed checkout quote (Q6), the return is the **token amount received**, so neither side gains or loses from price movement between charge and return. [CLEARANCE-CHECK: whether a same-asset return satisfies applicable consumer-refund law where the token's fiat value has moved is for the Q1 clearance; if any applicable right requires more, it applies in full and this term yields to it, on the pattern of §2.2.]

5.3 **Who bears the costs a return cannot recover — the proposed bounded amendment.** The original transaction's provider and network costs come off the top and are consumed when the transaction runs (§8.1); whether the provider returns its processing fee on a refund is not yet known (§5.4). A return of the whole amount taken can therefore exceed what the house retained. This policy allocates that gap expressly, and part of the allocation **amends the founder's No-Loss Rule** — it is written here as a proposal for his express approval, not as a drafting preference, and it has no force unless he approves it:

- **(a) The mistake was the town's** (a charge the till made in error, a duplicate the town's systems produced, a charge after refusal or withdrawal): the return is the whole amount taken, and the irrecoverable third-party costs of the original transaction are borne by the town — **this clause, and only this clause, is the proposed amendment**: a bounded exception to the No-Loss Rule, capped at the irrecoverable third-party costs of that one transaction, for town-error returns only. If the founder does **not** approve the amendment, the return in a town-error case is the amount taken minus those irrecoverable costs, with the exact figure stated to the buyer before the return is sent.
- **(b) The mistake was not the town's** (e.g. the buyer paid twice from his own wallet against the quote's stated terms): the return is the amount received, minus the return's own actual network/provider cost, with that cost stated to the buyer **before** the return is sent — never more than the actual cost, and never a fee for the return itself.
- **(c)** In every case, §7 stands above this section: any greater right the buyer holds under applicable law applies in full.

5.4 [CLEARANCE-CHECK: card and PayPal refund mechanics — provider timelines, whether the provider's original processing fee is returned on a refund, and chargeback rights — are set by provider terms and applicable law, not by this policy; the [7]-day figure above is the town's own service target for initiating and completing its side, and the final text must be conformed to the provider terms at clearance. The §5.3(a) amendment's size depends on this check: if providers return their fees on refund, the gap it covers may be network costs only.]

## §6 — PARTIAL DELIVERY

6.1 The fee is for a **completed** launch and is charged whole, at completion, or not at all. There is no partial fee. Work that stops short of the handover test — for any reason, on either side — has **no fee to return**, and the states of §2–§4 govern the ticket. No listing or quote may describe a partial-delivery fee or a partial refund of one.

## §7 — CONSUMER RIGHTS

7.1 Nothing in this policy limits any right a buyer holds under the law that applies to him. Where this policy and such a right differ, the right prevails and the policy is read subject to it. [CLEARANCE-CHECK: the jurisdictions whose consumer law applies to the town's sales, any cooling-off or digital-services rules, and any required pre-contract disclosures — all for the Q1 legal clearance. This draft asserts no conclusion on any of them.]

7.2 A buyer's ability to dispute a card or PayPal payment with his own provider is his provider's process; this policy neither grants nor restricts it. [CLEARANCE-CHECK: as §5.4.]

7.3 Crypto payments are made from the buyer's own wallet on his own signature. A return under this policy is a new payment by the town to the paying wallet, receipted in the Day-Book like every other state change. [CLEARANCE-CHECK: any reporting, tax, or money-transmission characterisation of such returns is for the Q1 clearance; nothing is characterised here.]

## §8 — HOW A RETURN UNWINDS THE SPLIT

8.1 The fee, when charged, is split 80/20 on **net receipts** — provider and network costs of the transaction come off the top first, covered by the transaction itself; only the remainder divides (founder's Q13 as amended by the No-Loss Rule).

8.2 A return under §3.2, §4.2, or §5 is funded by this **waterfall**, in this order, and the base of every step is stated so the arithmetic can always be re-walked:
1. **Recovered third-party amounts first** — any provider fee or other amount the rail returns to the town on the refund goes to the buyer (its size waits on the §5.4 clearance).
2. **The distributed net shares, recalled 80/20** — the founder's share and the town pot each give back in the same 80/20 proportion of the **net** amount they received, up to the full net amount.
3. **Any remainder** between the net amount and the whole amount taken — the original transaction's irrecoverable third-party costs — is borne **only** as §5.3 allocates it: by the town, if the §5.3(a) amendment is approved and the mistake was the town's; otherwise it is not added to the return, and the buyer is told the exact figure before the return is sent.
The unwinding is receipted in the same public reconciliation as the original split.

8.3 No return under this policy is ever funded from creator fees, the Golden Wall, or any other buyer's money.

## §9 — SCALING (per Listing Standard §8)

9.1 **Downloadable products:** delivery is the file manifest plus its documentation, complete at the receipt test (the buyer holds the files and the documentation that explains them). The same case structure applies: before delivery, nothing is held back because nothing beyond the ruled charge has been taken [CLEARANCE-CHECK: downloadable products may be charged at purchase rather than at a handover; if so, the cancellation and failed-delivery cases carry real content for them and the final text must state their timelines expressly]. A file that cannot be delivered at all is a failed delivery under §4's shape.

9.2 **Higher-ticket / Custom Build work:** the written quote and scope, approved before any charge, carry their own refund terms; the house's standing word for that door — *if we cannot build it, we say so plainly and charge nothing* (THE WHOLE HOUSE §4; quoted for that door in Listing Standard §6.5) — is a term of that door. This policy is the floor: no quote term may leave a buyer worse off than §§5 and 7 leave him.

9.3 **The Extended Audience** is unpriced and unsold until the founder sets its terms (Q9); when he does, its refund terms are set in the same act, on this policy's pattern.
