## §1 — PRODUCT EXPLANATION (Question 1: WHAT the customer is buying)

Every listing opens with the commercial word in plain language, in page text, before any purchase element. For the Coin Mint listing, the standard requires the following content, in this order:

**1.1 The offer, in one breath.** The town's mint service takes a non-technical founder from an idea to a launched coin — a "house" — under the town's mark, with the launch written into the town's public record. The town guides; the founder's own wallet creates, signs, and pays at every step. The town never holds keys, funds, or custody at any step, for any reason.

**1.2 Who it is for.** A person with an idea for a coin and no technical path to launch it — who wants the launch done in the open, with a record, and who is willing to hold his own keys and sign his own steps. It is equally for the person the service will decline: the Honest Question (§1.3, step 2) may answer "a coin is not the right tool," and that answer is a service outcome, not a failed sale.

**1.3 What happens, step by step.** The listing names the service's eight steps, in the service's own order (the same order the presentation explodes, §3):

1. **The Conversation** — the idea, in the founder's own words: what the coin is, why it should exist, who it is for.
2. **The Honest Question** — "Should This Be a Coin?" asked and answered together before anything is built; a *no* is the service working.
3. **The Rehearsal** — a guided, clearly labelled practice run (name, image, permissions, costs, custody, handover, what can go wrong). No seed phrase is ever collected. A rehearsal is never dressed as a real launch.
4. **The Covenant** — the house's commitments, read in full — not summarised — and signed. A signature qualifies, not a purchase.
5. **The Mark** — the house's image is prepared and the family mint mark is placed on it as the covenant requires.
6. **The Launch** — guided self-serve: the house teaches the clicks and never makes them. The founder's wallet is the coin's creator on the chain — never the town's wallet.
7. **The Record** — the house is entered in the town registry with its birth facts (mint, wallets, launch facts, disclosed buys), receipted in the Day-Book.
8. **The Handover** — the House Passport is issued, and the launch completes only when the founder can operate unaided (§6.3).

**1.4 What the buyer must provide.** His idea in his own words · a complete signed package — the specification's own list: the idea in the founder's own words, its foundation, a mint-marked image, the live conversation held, the signature given ("foundation" is the specification's term, defined no further in the record; its content is what the intake conversation establishes) — to receive a ticket · his own wallet and keys, held by himself throughout · the chain's own creation costs, paid from his own wallet directly to the chain at launch · true launch facts for the record · his presence when his ticket is called (inside the 72-hour response window, §6.4) · his own operation of the house after handover.

**1.5 What the buyer owns at the end (ownership terms).** The coin is the buyer's: his wallet is its creator on the chain; he holds its keys; he keeps **100% of his coin's creator fees, forever** — the town takes no percentage of any coin's economics. His image and source files are his (source exports handed over at birth). His House Passport is his portable dossier. His departure is founder-controlled: a house can leave the family and keep its coin and its records; leaving is recorded, not punished. What the town keeps is the public record itself — the registry entry, the Passport's facts, the Day-Book receipts are permanent — and the record states affiliation and facts, **not endorsement**: registration is not a quality mark.

**1.6 Limitations (stated on the listing, not buried).** The service is bounded so it can be honest: at most **2 build starts per month**, at most **1 new start per 7 days**, at most **5 active houses** at once; intake closes at **12 waiting tickets** and reopens below 6; one live ticket per person; tickets are non-transferable. **The intake gate is closed today** — no tickets are being issued (Mint Service Specification, gate status) — and the listing must say so in its status language (§5.4) rather than invite applications it cannot take.

**1.7 What the house never promises (printed on every listing).** No promise of promotion, volume, price, liquidity, market demand, or returns. The service builds a coin and a record; it does not market outcomes. The permanent honesty sentence appears verbatim: *"PROVADIA is a place, not an investment. Holding it names you a Bricklayer in the town registry and gives you a vote on the Wall's fate — and nothing else: no redemption, no yield, no rights, no expected return."* Holding PROVADIA is never an admission test, a priority, or a condition of this service in any amount (see Register item R-4 for the one open fork on this point).

## §2 — THE VALUE CASE (Question 2: WHY it is worth the price)

*Written in full, as the acceptance frame requires. Every claim below traces to the governing sources; the value case may not import a claim the sources do not make.*

**2.1 The problem.** Launching a coin is a technical act wrapped around irreversible ones. A non-technical founder working alone faces: seed phrases and custody — one mishandled phrase and the house is gone or stolen; permissions set wrong at creation that cannot be un-set; costs paid to the wrong place; no record of what was done, by whom, with what disclosed; and no one whose job is to ask whether a coin should exist at all. The house's own specification names the shape it refuses: a coin created desk-side, from the service's wallet, would pay its creator fees to the service forever — "the tithe rebuilt as plumbing," refused by design (Mint Service Specification, exclusions). A founder who cannot launch alone is pushed toward exactly that shape, or toward handing someone his keys. This service exists as the third option.

**2.2 What the price buys instead.** The $50 pilot (§4) buys the guided, custody-free version of the same launch:

- **The founder keeps everything that matters.** His wallet creates; his keys never leave his hands; the town never receives, fronts, holds, or co-signs his money; he keeps 100% of his coin's creator fees forever. The service's fee is the whole of what the town ever takes from the launch (with the chain's own costs paid by the founder directly to the chain — never through the town's hands).
- **The honest gate.** Step 2 exists to talk him out of it if a coin is the wrong tool. Declining an unsuitable launch is a service outcome, receipted like a birth — the reasoning recorded, the door left open (Mint Service Specification). The fee buys the gate's judgment, exercised in the open, not a guaranteed mint.
- **Rehearsal before irreversibility.** The practice run happens while mistakes are still free: permissions, costs, custody, handover, and failure modes, walked before the real signature.
- **A public, permanent record.** Registry entry, birth facts, disclosed buys, and the House Passport — what the house is, what was checked, what was handed over, what remains unverified, each in its own section. The record is the difference between a launch and a claim about a launch.
- **A finish line that is competence, not existence.** Handover completes when the founder can operate unaided — "your coin exists" is not handover; understanding what he owns is (§6.3).
- **Thirty days of full support after the birth** (§7.1), inside a queue whose caps (§1.6) exist so the service never sells attention it does not have.

**2.3 The value boundary (printed with the value case, always).** The value is the build, the guidance, and the record. It is **not** market value: no promotion, no volume, no price, no returns are promised or implied (§1.7), and a registered house is not an endorsed house. A buyer who wants exposure is buying the wrong thing, and the listing must be willing to say so before he pays, not after.

## §3 — VISUALS: THE PRESENTATION LEG (supports Questions 1–2: the demonstration)

The presentation is not decoration around the commercial word; it is the demonstration half of the value case — by the bottom of the scroll, the visitor knows every part of the service, how the parts connect, and what happens step by step. The binding source is the Presentation Standard; this section states its requirements as listing law.

**3.1 The journey.** One pinned hero, one journey, no cutting away: the product stays centre-stage for the whole scroll — whole → turns → comes apart layer by layer → reassembles — and the whole journey plays in **slow motion**: smooth, unhurried, however long it takes; seconds do not matter, the assembly must be *seen* (the founder's standing correction to this standard's source). The Mint specimen's arc, as graded: opens whole → turns and opens layer by layer → closes whole mid-film → rests open as the aligned named stack. One object, one axis, locked camera, no morphing; the page background equals the frames' background (sampled hex), so the product floats edge-free.

**3.2 Frames.** ~240 frames at 30 fps from an ~8-second film (the Presentation Standard's frame math: fps × seconds = frames; floor: no scroll page ships under 200 frames; the specimen carries 235). The standing media law separately describes "the scroll film itself" as 10–15 s of assembly motion; whether the two durations name one asset or two is not reconciled in the record — flagged at §10, F-9, registered at R-14, and the binding constraints until it is reconciled are the frame count, the budgets (§3.4), and the CWV bars (§3.5), which both readings must satisfy. Purpose-made matched pair: frame 1 = the whole thing; last frame = the same thing perfectly exploded and aligned; start/end prompts identical except the single state clause, the exploded prompt naming every part **in order** — those names are the callouts. Frames are drawn with canvas + drawImage (never img-src swapping, never video.currentTime scrubbing); raw scroll never touches the frame index — damping is mandatory (lerp chase k ≈ 0.1, time-corrected, or GSAP scrub ≥ 0.3 s); priority preload (frame 0 → last → midpoint → quartiles → fill) with nearest-loaded-frame fallback; lazy loading from ~120% of viewport; canvas DPR capped at 1.5–2; overlays animate transform and opacity only; scroll listeners passive + rAF.

**3.3 Bullets (the Bullet-Point Law).** As the product opens on scroll, a bullet appears beside each part/layer naming **what it is** and **what it does**, in that order, one line each, tied to that layer's scroll moment — transcribed from the exploded frame's part list, in its order, each with a **leader line + tip dot** to its part. Not a text block below the journey; the bullets ride the presentation. For the Mint listing, the eight bullets are the eight service steps of §1.3, in the service's order.

**3.4 Budgets (binding bars).** Frame sequence: **desktop ≤ 8.5 MB total · mobile ≤ 1.5 MB total**, the mobile set a true half-density set (the specimen's is 480w WebP), served to small screens — never step-sampled desktop files. (The Presentation Standard's craft rule ≈ ≤9 MB per sequence is noted; the stricter 8.5 MB gate governs — see §10, flag F-5.) Average frame ≤ ~40 KB at 960px WebP.

**3.5 Performance bars (CWV).** **LCP ≤ 2.5 s · INP < 200 ms · CLS < 0.1**, measured against the live page and filed — a gate is failed by an empty line, not by an honest number. Motion smoothness is judged on the founder's real device (Cloud-Browser Motion Law); a cloud check proves layout and statics, never motion.

**3.6 Supporting media (standing media law).** A gallery of 4–5 real images of the product itself · a UGC-style hook video (10–15 s) that earns attention in the first seconds — objects and places only, never synthetic people, never fake testimonials · the scroll film itself 10–15 s of real assembly motion (duration lines flagged at §10, F-9) · price in page text, never burned into visuals · 5–7 at-a-glance bullets · full description · deliverables list. Every asset's run report names which tool made it, from the new-tool stack named in the Presentation Standard (TRELLIS 2 / img2threejs for true 3D · rembg for clean cutouts · HyperFrames for composition · scroll-craft/GSAP patterns).

**3.7 Reduced motion and no-JS.** `prefers-reduced-motion` = static hero + stacked callouts: no pin, no scrub, no exceptions. A no-JS visitor receives the whole-product image plus the full commercial listing — the commercial word never depends on the film playing.

## §4 — PRICE (Question 3, first half)

**4.1 The price.** **$50 pilot, per completed launch** (the ruling's own basis: "On the proposed $50 pilot: $40 founder / $10 … per completed launch," founder rulings, 2026-11-21). "Completed" means the handover test of §6.3 is met. The price appears in the listing's page text — never burned into visuals — adjacent to what it buys (§1) and what it never buys (§1.7). Two things the record does **not** state, both registered rather than assumed: **when** the fee falls due (at scope confirmation, at the call, at completion — Register R-12), and the ticket is **issued free** (Mint Service Specification) — the pilot fee is a charge for the service, never for the ticket or the number.

**4.2 The split, stated on the listing.** Of the $50: **80% founder / 20% to the website and the community (the town pot)** — $40 / $10 — accounted in the same public reconciliation as creator fees. The listing states this split in its boundaries section. (The Golden Wall's 20% applies to creator fees → gold only; it does not touch service income. See §10, flag F-3.) **Flagged, not resolved:** when the buyer takes the §4.3 discount and pays $40, the record never states the split's base — 80/20 of the $40 actually paid ($32 / $8) or a fixed $10 to the pot. The same "$40" must never be printed doing both jobs; the base is Register R-13, and until it is ruled, the listing states the split as percentages of the amount paid and prints no dollar split beside a discounted price (see §10, flag F-8).

**4.3 The discount, in the listing's own words.** Every listing carries the discount block verbatim in substance: *"Pay in PROVADIA and take 20% off — the pilot is $40 in PROVADIA instead of $50. Card and PayPal pay the full $50; SOL pays the full price too, converted behind the counter."* The "$40" is a dollar price, not a token quantity: the PROVADIA amount is determined by the mechanism of Register R-6 — rate source, moment of fixing, and who bears movement between quote and payment are all unsettled until the founder rules them. The discount is flat, applies to the service price, and is never represented as an investment claim about the coin.

**4.4 What the price does not include.** The chain's own creation costs are not part of the $50 and are never paid to the town: the founder pays them in SOL, from his own wallet directly to the chain at launch (Mint Service Specification). The listing states this beside the price, so no buyer mistakes the pilot fee for an all-in cost. How a $50 price is converted into a PROVADIA amount at the moment of payment (rate source and moment) is **not settled in the record** — Register item R-6.

## §5 — PURCHASE REQUIREMENTS AND THE TILL (Question 3, second half: planned vs operational, honestly)

**5.1 The till's shape (PLANNED — not operational).** Everything in this section describes designed architecture; §5.3 states its true status, and no verb in this section may be read in the present tense of operation. The design has no separate payment page: each listing's buy area would be the till — **card (Stripe) · PayPal · crypto (SOL / PROVADIA)** side by side, through hosted checkout, ending at a thank-you page on our own site. Behind the counter, whatever rail the customer used, proceeds would be converted into PROVADIA at receipt or in a disclosed daily batch, and split per §4.2.

**5.2 What the buyer needs to purchase (when the till is live).** These requirements apply only after the till is wired, cleared, and announced live; they are not current requirements, and nothing in this section makes purchase possible today. A payment method on one of the three rails · for a crypto payment, his own wallet · the complete signed package of §1.4 for the ticket · his signature on the covenant (step 4) before any build step · scope confirmation for pilot work (the specimen's live wording, graded in Exam Artifact #1: "$50 pilot · scope confirmation required"). No PROVADIA holding is required to qualify (Register R-4 holds the open fork).

**5.3 The honest distinction (mandatory on every listing).** Documented architecture is not operational architecture. Today: the rails are **not connected, not legally cleared, not tested, and not operational**; the conversion and payout arrangements still need legal, technical, and financial verification before anyone treats them as operational (THE WHOLE HOUSE §6). Charges themselves go live only after the dated public notice changing the printed word "free" and the rehearsal validation (founder rulings, 2026-11-21; status of both conditions: not in the record — Register R-2). The purchase-and-delivery journey will first be verified end to end in an authorized test environment (sequence step 2) — this standard authorises no test and no transaction.

**5.4 The exact status language (verbatim block, carried by every listing while its till is unwired).** The buy area is rendered disabled (`data-purchase-enabled="false"`) and carries this text, unedited in substance:

> **"Checkout is not connected. This button does not book, purchase, or trigger fulfilment. The till opens only after the payment arrangements are legally cleared and the purchase-to-delivery journey has been proven in testing — and the house will say so here, in this same place, when it does. To ask about scope in the meantime, write to the house (support email, §7.2). A request is not a purchase and reserves nothing."**

For the Mint listing, one further line stands while the intake gate is closed (§1.6): *"The mint's intake gate is closed today. No tickets are being issued."*

**5.5 What never appears.** No countdown, no scarcity claim beyond the true caps of §1.6 stated as facts, no "buy now" affordance that performs a purchase the house cannot fulfil, and no language that represents a planned rail as a working one.

## §6 — DELIVERY TERMS (Question 4, first half)

**6.1 What is delivered.** The launched coin itself (created by the buyer's wallet, step 6) · the registry entry with birth facts, receipted in the Day-Book (step 7) · the House Passport (step 8): what the house is, what was checked, what was handed over, what remains unverified, each in its own section · the buyer's source exports (his image and source files) · the 30-day full-support window (§7.1).

**6.2 The form of delivery.** The coin exists on-chain under the buyer's own wallet — delivery is the guided launch and handover, not a shipment. The Passport and registry entry are the documentary delivery; the Day-Book receipts are the proof of each state change, timestamped and hash-chained in the registry book.

**6.3 When delivery is complete.** At handover, when the buyer can operate **unaided**: he holds his keys and has signed every step himself · he knows what his coin's permissions are — what he can change and what he cannot · he holds his Passport and source exports · he knows how to leave (departure is founder-controlled; a house can leave and keep its coin and records). "Your coin exists" is not delivery. Understanding what he owns is.

**6.4 Timeline language (no invented deadlines).** The listing promises **no birth date** — a promised date is never given at issue (Mint Service Specification). What the listing may state as fact: the queue's shape (his number, his state, every timestamp, how many stand ahead, and the desk's measured pace printed from the Day-Book) · the call rule (when his number is called, he has **72 hours** to respond; silence expires the ticket as a receipt, not a punishment, and re-application stands open at the back of the line; if the *town* misses its own slot, the founder keeps his priority) · the caps of §1.6. Any fixed per-launch delivery duration is **TO BE SET BY THE FOUNDER** — none exists in the record (Register R-5).

**6.5 If the house cannot deliver.** The ticket states name the failure modes honestly: **STOPPED** — the town starts a build and does not finish it — is receipted as the town's act, never dressed as founder silence; **REFUSED** applications keep their numbers with the refusal reason receipted; **LAPSED / WITHDRAWN / EXPIRED** are recorded as their own facts. The Custom Build door carries its own ruled word — *if we cannot build it, we say so plainly and charge nothing* (THE WHOLE HOUSE §4) — quoted here for that door only: it decides nothing about the Mint fee while R-7 is open, and no charged-service listing may carry it as, or let it be read as, a refund promise. The money must be described in its two separate flows, never merged: **(a) the chain's creation costs** are never the town's — they move from the founder's wallet directly to the chain, the town cannot return what it never held, and the listing says exactly that about chain costs and no more; **(b) the service fee**, once charges are live, *is* received by the town through the till — what happens to it on a decline after payment, a withdrawal, or a STOPPED build is **not settled in the record** and goes to the Register (R-7). Until R-7 is settled, no listing may state or imply a refund promise for the fee, and no listing may borrow flow (a)'s "never held" language to describe the fee.

## §7 — SUPPORT (Question 4, second half)

**7.1 The included window.** Full support runs from the start of the build through **30 days after the house is born**: questions answered, the record checked together, mistakes corrected in the open. After day 30 the house stands on its own; ongoing operation is the founder's. The registry entry, the Passport, and the public record are permanent. No response-time promise (hours, same-day, or otherwise) exists in the record — a listing states the window and the doors below, and no invented SLA (Register R-8).

**7.2 The doors.** (a) **The Keeper at the counter** — Solnik, the town's host and shopkeeper: counter help is free; by design he presents any product, explains it, and walks a guest to the till — **OFFLINE today; see the mandatory status at §7.3.** (b) **The town's support email**, as printed on the Agora page — live today. (c) **The Agora** itself — the town's public square, where a question can be put in the open (membership stands at zero today; until the square is alive, door (b) is the working support door).

**7.3 The Keeper's status, as every listing must state it.** Wherever a listing describes Keeper support, it carries the status in the same breath, in substance: *"The Keeper's counter is being built. Solnik is OFFLINE today — the 24/7 resident claim is published only when the host truly serves. Until then, the support email (§7.2) is the working door."* No listing may describe the Keeper as presently available around the clock (THE WHOLE HOUSE §8).

**7.4 The Extended Audience boundary.** Extended project work with the Keeper — beyond counter help and beyond the included window — is the paid **Extended Audience** tier. Its price and terms are **not in the record** (Register R-9); until the founder sets them, no listing quotes an Extended Audience price, and the boundary is described, not sold.

## §8 — SCALING THE STANDARD (Question 5: all 35 listings)

**8.1 One skeleton, variable fields.** The per-type adaptations in §§8.2–8.4 are this standard's template definitions — the standard's own design work, not claims about any existing listing — and each is confirmed by the founder when the first listing of its type is audited against §9. Every town listing is built on this standard's fixed skeleton — (i) the commercial word (§1 shape: offer, who for, steps or contents, buyer provides, ownership, limitations, never-promises) · (ii) the value case (§2 shape: problem, what the price buys, value boundary) · (iii) the presentation leg (§3) · (iv) price + split + discount (§4) · (v) purchase requirements + till + status language (§5) · (vi) delivery terms (§6) · (vii) support (§7) · (viii) the honesty sentences (§1.7). What varies per listing is field content, never the skeleton and never the honesty requirements.

**8.2 Services** (the Mint; the Resident done-for-you form; any offers the founder later stocks in the Studio — a town door that stands "not yet open" today): steps replace "contents"; delivery is the performed work ending at a defined handover test; the queue/caps language applies where the service is bounded.

**8.3 Downloadable products** (the Resident kit form; digital deliverables generally): "what the buyer receives" is a file manifest delivered as the listing states; "installation" language replaces the launch steps (what the files need, what the buyer installs or opens, what documentation ships inside); the handover test becomes a receipt test — the buyer holds the files and the documentation that explains them. Delivery timeline language stays inside §6.4's rule: only what the record supports, no invented promises.

**8.4 Higher-ticket offerings** (the Custom Build door; the higher-ticket class the founder's standing direction names — $100/$200/$500/$1,000 tools and offers, ruled 2026-11-15): the skeleton holds, with the quote variant — written quote and scope approved before any charge; if the house cannot build it, it says so and charges nothing. The value case (§2) carries more weight as price rises: the demonstration and the boundary must be proportionally fuller, and the open-decisions discipline tightens — no term a higher-ticket listing needs may be invented to fill its page; it goes to the Register and the listing waits, or ships without the term stated as a question the house answers before charge.

**8.5 What never scales away.** The presentation standard binds every listing without exception ("no exceptions, coin services included"). The disabled-until-cleared status language (§5.4) binds every listing whose till is unwired — today, all of them. The completeness checklist (§9) is the audit instrument for each of the other 34, applied one listing at a time, in the founder's sequence.

## §9 — THE COMPLETENESS CHECKLIST (the audit instrument)

**Presentation**
6. ☐ Pinned centre journey in slow motion: whole → turn → layer-by-layer → reassemble; one object, one axis, locked camera; background matched.

## §10 — CONTRADICTIONS IDENTIFIED (flagged, never silently resolved)

- **F-1 · Free vs charged.** The Mint Service Specification's body (founder ruling, 2026-11-21, §2C CLOSED) rules the service **free** — houses pay only chain costs.
