## ITEM 1 — N1 · T9 burst vs. §6.2 quota

**Certainty: CONFIRMED.**
**Anchors:** §6.2 (existing, append) · §8/T9 (existing, replace in full).

**Amendment 1a — §6.2, append precedence clause:**
> The quota stated in this section is a law of the Heart, not a default. It binds every emitter, every test procedure, every threshold, every worked example, and every read model. Any text in this specification that assumes an acceptance rate above this quota is invalid. Where any provision of this specification conflicts with §6.2, §6.2 governs.

**Amendment 1b — §8, T9 replaced:**
> **T9 — QUOTA LAW UNDER BURST.** Purpose: prove that §6.2's quota governs under burst load. Procedure: submit 100 valid envelopes from one emitter within a single 60-second window. Expected: (a) exactly 10 envelopes are accepted (which 10 is implementation-defined; the count is not); (b) exactly 90 envelopes are rejected with the quota-rejection signal; (c) the 90 rejections are correct behavior and count toward PASS, not FAIL; (d) each accepted envelope yields exactly one beat per the definition in DEF-1; (e) the Heart's acceptance rate never exceeds the §6.2 quota at any instant in the window. PASS: (a)–(e) all hold. Note: 100/minute is the test's *input load profile*, not an expected capacity; expected capacity is the §6.2 quota. Nothing in T9 may be read as an exception to §6.2.

**Reason:** v0.2's T9 assumed 100 events/minute against §6.2's 10/minute quota — a confirmed self-contradiction. The build ruled and verified: the quota governs (100 envelopes → 10 accepted, 90 correctly rejected). The amendment states which number governs (the quota), demotes the burst to a load profile, and converts T9 from a contradiction into the quota law's proving test.

**Downstream:** T9 procedure and pass criteria; read models must expose envelopes-seen / accepted / quota-rejected as three distinct counters (100 / 10 / 90 under T9 load); all rate thresholds and alerts must derive from the quota, never from burst magnitude; sweep v0.2 for other burst-assumption text (register #2).

## ITEM 2 — N3 · "beat" overloaded (T1 journey vs. QUIET glow)

**Certainty: CONFIRMED** (definition/canon — adopted in the build, T1 passing under it). **NEEDS-CHECK** on completeness of the term sweep (register #1).
**Anchors:** DEF-1 (Definitions — new entry) · QUIET state description (existing, edit) · §8/T1 (existing, clarify).

**Amendment 2a — DEF-1, new definition:**
> **beat** (*n.*): one complete stream journey of exactly one accepted record — from acceptance at the Heart's intake to its final resting place in the stream. One accepted record yields exactly one beat; nothing else yields a beat. Envelopes rejected at any gate (quota, validation, or otherwise) yield no beat. The QUIET state's resting glow is explicitly NOT a beat: it is a presentation state of the Heart at rest and must never be counted, logged, metered, or tested as a beat.

**Amendment 2b — QUIET description, edit:** replace every use of "beat" denoting the glow with "resting glow," and append: "The resting glow is presentation only; it is not a beat, and no beat is derived from it."

**Amendment 2c — §8/T1, clarify:** retitle "T1 — ONE BEAT, ONE JOURNEY" and append: "T1 is the canonical use of the term 'beat'; all other uses in this specification defer to DEF-1."

**Reason:** v0.2 used "beat" for two different things. The build canon — one beat = one stream journey per accepted record; the glow is not a beat — is verified in code. v0.3 defines the term once and enforces consistency.

**Downstream:** T1 (terminology anchor; no behavior change); QUIET prose (terminology only); any metric or read-model field named "beats" must derive from accepted-record journeys, never from glow state; glossary gains the entry.

## ITEM 3 — N4 · shell independence is architectural, not a test accident

**Certainty: CONFIRMED** (build fact: static shell served separately; verified with the Heart service stopped). **NEEDS-CHECK** on sweep for any v0.2 prose implying shell-served-by-Heart (register #6).
**Anchors:** ARCH-1 (Architecture — new subsection) · §8/T11 (existing, restate).

**Amendment 3a — ARCH-1, new requirement:**
> **ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT — SHELL/SERVICE SEPARATION.** The demo shell MUST be a static artifact served from an origin independent of the Heart service. Shell reachability MUST NOT depend on the Heart service process being up. When the Heart service is stopped, crashed, or restarting, the shell MUST still load and present its defined offline state. This separation is a construction requirement: a shell served by, proxied through, or process-coupled to the Heart service violates this section regardless of what any test run observes. No implementation may rely on caching, routing luck, or any incidental mechanism for shell availability.

**Amendment 3b — §8/T11 replaced:**
> **T11 — SHELL INDEPENDENCE.** *Verifies* ARCH-1; does not define it. Procedure: with the Heart service fully stopped, load the shell. PASS: the shell loads from its independent origin and presents its offline state. A load achieved by cache, proxy residue, or any coupling to the Heart process is a FAIL of ARCH-1 even if the page appears.

**Reason:** v0.2 stated independence only as a test assumption; the build makes it structural. Promoting it to an architectural requirement prevents a refactor from silently regressing the property while still passing a shallow test.

**Downstream:** T11 pass criterion (now cites ARCH-1 and fail-closes cache-luck); deployment/topology description of the shell origin.

## ITEM 5 — D6 · 60-second individual-record delay

**Certainty: CONFIRMED** (standing decision, build-enforced; codified here as normative text). **NEEDS-CHECK** on the reference instant if house canon differs from acceptance (register #5).
**Anchor:** RM-1 (read-model / public-output section — new subsection).

**Amendment 5:**
> **D6 — INDIVIDUAL RECORD DELAY.** Any public read path that exposes an individual record MUST withhold that record for 60 seconds from its acceptance (the start of its beat). Aggregate signals — pulse, counts, rates — are not individual records and are not subject to the delay. No read path may expose an individual record in real time; no parameter, mode, or debug flag may bypass the delay.

**Reason:** D6 is standing and enforced in code, but a law that lives only in code can be dropped by a future implementation. This makes the delay normative for read models, the shell's record views, and all visibility tests.

**Downstream:** read models serving individual records (60-second buffer); the shell's "recent records" presentation (delayed or aggregate-only); any v0.2 test asserting real-time visibility of an individual record contradicts D6 and must be corrected (register #1 sweep); public-visibility metrics.

## ITEM 6 — D7 · founder-countersigned registry changes

**Certainty: CONFIRMED** (directly observed: the prototype refuses to boot on an unsigned registry edit).
**Anchor:** REG-1 (registry section — new subsection).

**Amendment 6:**
> **D7 — REGISTRY COUNTERSIGNATURE.** The registry may be changed only by a founder-countersigned offline artifact. The Heart MUST refuse to boot if the on-disk registry does not carry a valid founder countersignature; unsigned or tampered registries fail closed. There is no override, no runtime registry mutation path, and no environment bypass. Verified in the build: the prototype refuses to boot on an unsigned registry edit.

**Reason:** D7 is standing and the boot-refusal is observed behavior. Normative text binds registry tooling, deployment runbooks, and test fixtures — and E5.1 at G3, since emitter expansion multiplies registry changes; the countersignature law must be spec-grade before expansion begins.

**Downstream:** registry tooling and runbook text (countersign step mandatory); registry test fixtures must be countersigned TEST artifacts (D3-compatible); E5.1 cites D7; any admin/ops text promising runtime registry edits must be removed (register #6).
