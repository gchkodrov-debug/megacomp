## A · RECOMMENDATIONS — THE STRONGEST DISTRIBUTION DESIGN

### A.1 The shape in one paragraph

One edition, two releases. The **Pilot (47 pieces)** runs first, under the *full* adopted gate — no weaker pilot gate — to the first members who genuinely qualify. Then a **pre-defined engagement examination** (A.3) is run and reported to the founder and both partners. Only on the founder's word after that examination does the **Full Release (remaining 423)** open. The edition closes permanently at #470 or the coin's first birthday (**2027-11-18**), when the mint authority is revoked — pilot or no pilot, that date does not move.

### A.2 The Pilot — who the first 47 are for, sequencing, and how claims and the ceremony actually work

- **Who:** the founding cohort — the first members to pass the complete Three-Lock Gate (§B) once claims open. No invitations list, no insider allocation, no reserved serials: the pilot is not a separate class of member, it is simply the first 47 successful claims under the same rules everyone else will face. This is deliberate: a pilot run under special rules would prove nothing about the real release.
- **Serials:** drawn by lot from the privately held shuffled pool (Privacy Amendment v1.1), exactly as in the full release. The pilot does **not** take serials #001–#047 in order — chronological or low-number allocation would create a visible "early = special" hierarchy the privacy design exists to prevent, and would hand flippers a numbered target. The pool itself is the locked, shuffled permutation of §C item 8.
- **Claims execute immediately (v0.2, hardening H6 — this paragraph replaces v0.1's contradictory cadence text):** *On-chain claims execute immediately upon passing the Three-Lock Gate. Solnik's Agora Welcome Ceremony convenes weekly as a shared celebration for that week's newly minted Bricklayers. The ceremony celebrates; it does not gate.* A member who qualifies claims in the same session — the house never holds a member's earned claim hostage to a schedule — and is *welcomed* at that week's ceremony.
- **The ceremony (design summary; build spec at §C item 7):** an **individual** interactive cinematic rite conducted by Solnik for each new Bricklayer, paired with an **asynchronous cohort roll-call plaque** in the Agora. The plaque is **aggregate or optional-pseudonym, EXPLICITLY OPT-IN** (v0.3, K5): by default it records that week's cohort as an aggregate ("this week, N new Bricklayers were welcomed"); a member appears under a self-chosen pseudonym **only if they opt in**; and the plaque **never links citizenship to token, serial or wallet address** — the two objects stay unjoined in every field the plaque holds. It requires **no real-time multiplayer** — MES-SHR-001/002 (multi-visitor presence, shared world state) are MISSING in Inventory v0.1, and Genesis does not stall behind unbuilt networking infrastructure.
- **Sequencing (dependency order, no step skippable):**
  1. The **Atomic Pre-Registration Perimeter** (§C items 1–5, widened in v0.3 per K3 — **real verified email delivery is inside the perimeter**, because recovery depends on delivered mail) closed and verified — only then does *registration* open. §C item 6 (real OAuth) is verified before the first claim is evaluated; no claim can be evaluated against a stub. **Staged/test deployment under §C is not launch:** public launch authorization is a **separate founder act** (§F item 6), distinct from every verification step in this sequence.
  2. **§F threshold monitoring built and TESTED** (§C item 10): a simulated breach of each of the five thresholds is demonstrated flipping the pause switch. No public claim exists before this test passes (ChatGPT refinement C5).
  3. Founder's hands §F items 3–5 done (relayer wallet · legal checklist · his own devnet ceremony inspection).
  4. Mainnet collection created under the Relayer Safeguard (§C item 9) — setup **reserved against the Cumulative Pilot Spend Ledger before signing** (v0.3, K1), paid from the founder's cold/creation wallet and settled in the ledger at actual cost — with `GENESIS_CLAIM_ENABLED=false`; production metadata uploaded in the independent upload order and audited (§C item 8, MES-GEN-007).
  5. Real members exist who have run the 7-day clock and completed a genuine deed (the registry stands at **0 members** today; the earliest possible pilot start is 7 days after the first real members qualify — this cannot be shortcut, and the strategy does not pretend otherwise).
  6. Founder gives the DEPLOY GENESIS word → pilot claims open, executing immediately under the atomic cap (C1) and the cumulative budget (C2).
- **Pilot communications rule:** all public copy about the pilot carries the no-promises language of §E.5 verbatim. The pilot is announced as a founding-citizenship honour, never as an asset opportunity.

### A.3 The engagement examination — measurable objectives, defined BEFORE launch, judged on FULL observation

These six metrics **are** the pilot's measurable community-engagement objectives (founder's requirement, 2026-11-26): named now, with their thresholds, before the pilot opens, so nobody can move the goalposts after seeing the numbers.

**Definitions (v0.3, K7 — named here so no metric can be re-based after the fact):**
- **Eligible cohort (the uptake denominator):** the members who **passed the full Three-Lock Gate during the pilot window and remained eligible at its close**. Metric 1's percentage is computed against this cohort and no other — not against all registrants, not against all members.
- **Uptake window:** **14 days** from the moment a member passes the gate. A claim inside the window counts toward uptake; a claim after it does not (it still counts toward the 47, and toward that claimant's own retention window).
- **Pilot claiming window:** opens at the founder's DEPLOY GENESIS word (§F item 6) and closes when the 47th claim lands **or 60 days after opening, whichever comes first** — the **incomplete/stop rule** below.

**Observation rule (v0.2, ChatGPT refinement C3 · sharpened v0.3, K7):** every participant receives a **full observation period**. Each claimant's retention is measured over their own complete 30 days after claiming. The examination window therefore closes **30 days after the 47th pilot claim lands** — the moment the last participant's full window has run — and the report is compiled only then. **Incomplete/stop rule:** if the 47th claim has not landed within the 60-day claiming window, **claiming stops** and the pilot's status is **INCOMPLETE**; observation of the members who did claim still runs in full (the stop truncates claiming, never observation), and the report to the founder says exactly that, with the claim count and the reason. **No success may be declared until full mature observation completes** — for a complete pilot, that is 30 days after the 47th claim; for an incomplete one, there is no success to declare at any point. **ALL intermediate metric values read HOLD:** before the examination closes, any interim reading of any metric is reported as **HOLD** — never as GO, never as a passing trend — so no partial number can be quoted as evidence the pilot is "working." An incomplete pilot is never declared successful, and the 423 do not release on an incomplete examination. **Report:** captain compiles, both partners challenge, founder decides. **Decision rule:** the remaining 423 release **only** on the founder's explicit word after this report — silence or a mixed result means the pilot stays the whole release until he rules.

### B.1 · Eligibility examinations (founder-ordered 2026-11-26 12:21 CT — three questions, examined in v0.1, hardened by the partner examinations in v0.2)

**Kept without examination (founder's invariants):** the 7-day account age · the genuine-participation deed · one per member, ever · the house pays all costs within the approved budget (§D) · the 25-mints/day relayer safety cap. ChatGPT's examination **accepted** all three recommendations below; the second examination concurred **with hardenings**, which are now part of the design. Each item remains subject to the founder's final approval (§F item 1).

**Examination (a) — Must social login (Google/X OAuth) be MANDATORY, or is verified email enough?**
*The tension (from v0.1):* the OAuth leg is real anti-Sybil strength, but mandating it excludes a legitimate person with email and no Google/X account — and PROVADIA's privacy posture argues for collecting the *least* identity that does the job. *The hardening's counterweight (H3):* dropping OAuth entirely drops the Sybil identity cost to a $1 catch-all domain plus two machine-generated Agora comments, bypassing static disposable-email lists.
**v0.2 DESIGN: email-verification mandatory; social login OPTIONAL — with a TWO-TRACK DEED.** OAuth stays the encouraged, faster path (Track 1, standard deed). Email-only members are fully welcome (Track 2) but prove participation at a higher, time-spread bar (purchase, or 3 posts across ≥ 2 days + a Book of Wants entry). MX/catch-all domain scoring closes the $1-domain hole: catch-all addresses are Track 2 for deed purposes regardless of OAuth. The deed — not the login method — remains the participation proof on both tracks; the tracks differ in how much proof, because the identity evidence differs. (§B Lock 3 carries the operative text; §C item 6's acceptance test reads "OAuth available and verified where used," not "OAuth required.")

**Examination (b) — Does the ≥ 0.005 SOL wallet minimum unnecessarily bar legitimate members from a FREE giveaway?**
*The tension (from v0.1):* the minimum exists to prove a real, active wallet and to add farming friction — but the giveaway's whole point is that it costs the member nothing, and a brand-new member with an empty wallet is plausibly exactly the newcomer this community initiative exists to welcome. The house relayer pays the mint; the member's balance plays no functional role in the claim. *The hardening's counterweight (H4):* a fresh keypair costs $0 and takes milliseconds; with 0.000 SOL and no prior transactions there is no upstream funder, which blinds funding-graph Sybil detection (metric 2, §F-1).
**v0.2 DESIGN: the balance minimum is REMOVED; ownership is proven by signed challenge (C4); and detection stops depending on the funding graph.** MES-GEN-006's signal set is extended beyond funder clustering with **account-creation/deed timing correlation** (accounts created and deed-farmed in lockstep), **session/ASN clustering** (shared network fingerprints across supposedly distinct members), and **burst-claim detection** (claim velocity shapes no organic cohort produces). These signals work on zero-SOL wallets precisely because they measure behaviour, not balances. Solnik's Chamber guides the zero-SOL newcomer (connect-and-sign, seed phrases never touched, §B). If the pilot examination (§A.3, metric 2) shows Sybil pressure the design did not predict, the founder can reinstate a minimum for the full release with evidence in hand — a reversible decision, taken on data.

**Examination (c) — The 1-claim-per-IP rule vs legitimate households.**
*The flaw (from v0.1):* as a hard lock, "max 1 claim / IP / 24h" punishes exactly the wrong people — households, dorms, workplaces, cafés and carrier-grade NAT neighbourhoods sharing one public IP — while a determined farm rotates IPs trivially. *The hardening's counterweight (H5):* a single 24h step-up window can itself be gamed — 3 claims every 24h 01m across the 7-day qualifying period yields 21 claims from one IP without ever triggering a 4th-in-24h step-up.
**v0.2 DESIGN: IP is a SIGNAL with DUAL ROLLING WINDOWS, never a lock.** (i) Claims from one IP are *counted and scored*, never refused on IP alone. (ii) **Step-up triggers at ≥ 3 distinct-member claims from one IP within 24 hours OR ≥ 5 within 7 days** — the second window closes the slow-drip gap. (iii) **Subnet soft-scoring:** /24 (IPv4) and /48 (IPv6) aggregates feed the risk score below the step-up threshold. (iv) Step-up = a fresh Turnstile challenge + human moderation review of the deed — never a refusal. (v) A claim is refused on velocity grounds only when the IP signal *combines* with another independent signal (confirmed wallet cluster, synchronized deed timing, disposable/catch-all email pattern). (vi) The registration-side 1/IP/24h likewise becomes a step-up trigger, not a wall. (vii) **IPs are stored only as salted HMAC-SHA256 hashes — never as raw IP strings** (Privacy Amendment v1.1); velocity counters operate on the hashes. (viii) Every velocity decision is logged with its signals, so the pilot examination can audit false positives by name. The B9 acceptance test (§C item 4) proves all directions: a scripted farm burst is caught, a slow-drip pattern trips the 7-day window, **and** a simulated household of four distinct genuine members on one IP all claim successfully (ChatGPT C6).

### ⭐ THE CUMULATIVE PILOT SPEND LEDGER + PROSPECTIVE SPEND RESERVATION (v0.2, ChatGPT refinement C2 · enforcement made PROSPECTIVE in v0.3, K1)

ChatGPT's examination named the flaw in v0.1 plainly: *a wallet holding no more than 0.25 SOL is not itself a spending ceiling if it can be replenished.* Correct — v0.1's "the pilot physically cannot spend money that is not in the relayer" was true of a moment and false of a budget. v0.2 therefore enforced the pilot budget **cumulatively**. The byte examination then named v0.2's own flaw just as plainly: a cumulative ledger that records spend *after* it happens is **retrospective** — between the last check and the next signature, parallel claims can collectively promise more than the cap holds. **v0.3 therefore enforces the cap prospectively, by reservation:**

- **The ledger:** an append-only **Cumulative Pilot Spend Ledger** records **every** pilot-attributable outflow — entry #1 is the one-time setup (~0.045 SOL, paid from the founder's cold/creation wallet), and every claim's mint + priority fee thereafter — each entry carrying its transaction signature, so the ledger is reconcilable against the chain. Every entry exists in one of two states: **RESERVED** (promised, not yet settled) or **SETTLED** (its actual final cost, confirmed on-chain).
- **Reserve-before-signing (the binding rule):** before **any** signature — a claim mint, a setup upload, a retry — the machinery **atomically reserves the operation's maximum all-in expense**: for a claim, the mint cost at the signer's worst case (the ≤ 0.008 SOL priority-fee cap of H2 plus the base mint cost); for setup, the quoted upload + collection cost; **pending attempts and failed-transaction fees reserve against the same ledger** — a failed transaction's fee is a real outflow and is never treated as free. The reservation is taken in the same database transaction as the claim reservation (§C item 9), or not at all.
- **The invariant, in integer lamports:** at every reservation, **`spent_lamports + reserved_lamports + max_next_lamports ≤ 250_000_000`** (0.25 SOL). If the invariant would break, the reservation — and therefore the signature — is **refused**. No sequence of parallel claims, retries or fee spikes can promise past the cap, because the promise itself is what is capped.
- **Settlement:** only **actual final costs settle**, after on-chain confirmation. On settlement the reservation converts to a settled entry at the true cost and any unused remainder is released — **on settlement, never before**; an ambiguous outcome keeps its reservation standing until authoritative reconciliation (§C item 9, K2).
- **Replenishment changes nothing:** refilling the relayer float restores the hot wallet's working balance; it does **not** reset, extend or dilute the ledger — **spent and reserved both count against the cap, and refills never reset the cumulative ledger**, for the life of the pilot.
- **The cap:** cumulative pilot spend (settled + reserved) is hard-capped at **0.25 SOL total**. When reservations can no longer fit under the invariant, new claims **pause automatically** (the same pause switch, §E.4). Expected spend at study rates is ~0.184 SOL, leaving ~0.066 SOL of headroom for fee variance — variance the ≤ 0.008 SOL single-transaction priority-fee cap (H2) and the rolling-20 pause (§F-3) further bound.
- **Acceptance tests for the reservation mechanism (all five mandatory):** (i) **just-below-cap** — with the ledger at 0.25 SOL minus less than one claim's maximum, the next reservation is refused and nothing signs; (ii) **parallel claims** — a burst of simultaneous reservations can never make `spent + reserved` exceed 250_000_000 lamports, at any interleaving; (iii) **failed fees** — an authoritatively-failed transaction settles at its actual fee, releases only the unused remainder, and the fee stays counted; (iv) **restart** — a relayer/gate restart with reservations outstanding loses none, duplicates none, and releases none early; (v) **setup overrun** — if the setup's actual cost exceeds its estimate, the overrun settles against the ledger, minting headroom shrinks by exactly the overrun, and had the setup reservation itself breached the invariant it would not have proceeded.
- **Raising the cap is a founder act:** any spend beyond 0.25 SOL cumulative requires a **new written founder approval** naming the new cap. The pilot does not drift into the full-release budget on its own; the remaining 423 claims (~1.248 SOL derived) are a separate budget, unlocked only by the founder's post-examination ruling (§F item 7) and recorded as a new ledger section — under the same reservation discipline — when he rules.
- **Coverage disclosure (plain, and binding on how this section may be quoted):** the 0.25 SOL cap and the lamport invariant govern **on-chain pilot exposure only** — setup uploads, mints, priority fees and failed-transaction fees paid by the house wallets. **Non-chain costs are disclosed separately** in the table above (hosting: no new line item recorded · Turnstile, email delivery, execution-time Arweave pricing, legal: UNVERIFIED where marked) and are **not** inside the invariant. **No claim of full exposure coverage is made anywhere in this strategy.**

**Monitoring scope (v0.2, H1):** MES-GEN-006's rolling-20 average is computed **strictly over per-claim mint + priority-fee transaction costs**. The setup transaction is excluded by construction — it is paid from a different wallet, recorded in the ledger, and never enters the per-claim average. (In v0.1's unscoped design, setup landing in the average would have read Claim #1 as (0.045 + 0.00295) / 1 = 0.04795 SOL > the 0.015 SOL pause line and tripped the pause on the very first mint. That failure mode is designed out.)

**Budget shape for the founder's decision:** the pilot's entire **on-chain** exposure is **~0.184 SOL expected (~$33 at the study's reference rate), hard-capped at 0.25 SOL cumulative by the ledger and its reservation invariant**, with the relayer float (≤ 0.25 SOL) standing separately as a theft/drain bound on the hot wallet. That is the whole *on-chain* financial risk of testing the strategy; the non-chain items disclosed in the table are additional, mostly UNVERIFIED, and are **not** claimed to be covered by the cap.
