## 2. Seven corrections Gemini must address

### Correction 1 — Mobile performance is not guaranteed
Gemini claims that a 1.5 MB scene budget guarantees 60 FPS on ordinary phones. That is incorrect. Download size, decoded memory, GPU workload, animation complexity and thermal performance are different measurements. For example, 240 decoded frames at 540 × 960 pixels could occupy approximately **475 MiB of memory** if held simultaneously as uncompressed RGBA images. We must compare hardware-decoded video against WebP frame sequences on physical phones before selecting the permanent format.

### Correction 2 — The world needs genuine spatial depth
Gemini's depth-projection proposal is promising, but a depth map cannot reconstruct everything hidden behind objects in a single picture. When the visitor walks around furniture, turns a corner or approaches a doorway, missing geometry may become visible. We need genuine spatial geometry wherever the visitor interacts with the environment. Cinematic scenery can provide the surrounding beauty.

### Correction 3 — Do not manufacture 1,350 videos prematurely
Gemini proposes spending 13,500 credits before December 5. That represents approximately 135 generations per day over ten days. [Gemini's proposal: 19,713 credits — Scenery 6,500 · Journeys 5,000 · Characters and crowds 2,000 · Omni performances 2,400 · Remaining production buffer 3,813 — **not authorized expenditure**.] I agree that expiring credits should be used intelligently. However, generating hundreds of assets before proving the engine could create hundreds of incompatible assets. **Prove the manufacturing specification first, then accelerate aggressively.**

### Correction 4 — Automation percentages need evidence
Gemini estimates 90% post-production automation and 70% assisted prompt preparation. Those are useful targets, not established results. I want a timed production trial measuring human involvement, generation time, rejection rate, editing time and integration time.

### Correction 5 — Google's limitations require confirmation
The reported Omni duration restriction is credible because the founder encountered it directly. However, Gemini's explanation involving the model's internal memory window is not established by the error message alone. Similarly, the 16–24-second Veo continuity limit should be treated as a proposed production rule, not a universal technical limit. The reported December 5 retirement and December 23 credit expiration dates must also be confirmed against the actual accounts and current Google notices.

### Correction 6 — Ownership does not mean immortality
Owning the software and exporting the cinematic assets are essential. However, the software will still require maintenance, compatible browsers, hosting, security updates and functioning intelligence services. We must engineer provider independence rather than promise that everything will operate forever without maintenance.

### Correction 7 — Preserve the founder's complete vision
Gemini's recommendation of curated camera perspectives is acceptable for cinematic composition. It is not acceptable if it removes the visitor's ability to genuinely explore. The visitor must control the journey. The movie must respond to the visitor — not the other way around.
