# GEMINI'S SEVEN BLIND SPOTS — VERIFICATION CHECK (2026-11-25)

**Seat:** blind-spot verification (captain's crew). **Source of the seven:** Gemini's G-2 judgment on the Lantern Archive examination — seven silent failure modes he ordered inspected before any geometry helper is ported into the house engine.
**Surfaces:** S1 = the extracted Lantern specimen (`vibe-experiment-exam/src/`) · S2 = house World Engine + geometry (`construction/world-engine/`) · S3 = Avatar System locomotion + companion (`construction/avatar-system/`) · S4 = the prototype journey (`prototype-journey/`), the shipping reference.
**Verdicts:** CONFIRMED-PRESENT (the defect exists) / ABSENT (handled — code cited) / NOT-APPLICABLE (no such surface — reason given). Verification only; no code was changed.

**Scorecard up front:** against the specimen (S1), five of Gemini's seven come back ABSENT — his own build already clamps dt, caps DPR, slides, stores world-space coordinates, and cleans up. One is CONFIRMED on both halves (№7: reduced motion + the fonts call). Against OUR code the list earns its keep differently: one latent gap (№1 in S3), one real defect (№5 in S4), and a set of obligations that land on the Wave-2 renderer/locomotion seats because S2/S3 deliberately own no screen, no canvas and no input.

## №1 — Unclamped rAF delta-time

- **S1 — ABSENT.** The main loop clamps before anything moves: `const dt = Math.min(0.05, Math.max(0.001, (now - lastTimestamp) / 1000))` (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:280`). The modal's particle loop clamps too (`StarCompassModal.tsx:108`). Movement is velocity × real elapsed time with exponential damping (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:382, 394-395`), so a 120 Hz screen halves dt and changes nothing; a frame drop can never inject more than 50 ms of motion.
- **S2 — ABSENT.** The master clock is the clamp: `const dt = lastMs === null ? 0.016 : Math.min(0.05, Math.max(0, (nowMs - lastMs) / 1000))` (`world-engine.js:409`), documented at `world-engine.js:404-405` as the prototype's tab-switch guard.
- **S3 — CONFIRMED-PRESENT (latent, low severity).** `update(dt)` floors dt at zero but applies **no upper clamp**: `const stepDt = Math.max(0, dt || 0)` (`avatars.js:144`; same pattern `companion.js:116`). Two mitigations keep this from being Gemini's failure mode: dt is real elapsed time (never frame-count), so 120 Hz cannot double speed; and the step is distance-capped — `Math.min(dist, this.def.speed * this.speedScale * stepDt)` — so an oversized dt can only arrive early, never overshoot or tunnel past the target. The real residual risk: a host feeding raw rAF dt (tab-switch gap of seconds) makes a walker *teleport-arrive* instead of walking. The module is safe only under its intended drivers — the S2 clock and the S4 loop, both clamped — and does not enforce that contract itself.
- **S4 — ABSENT.** `const dt = Math.min(0.05, lastTs ? (ts - lastTs) / 1000 : 0.016)` (`journey.js:492`), in the single engine loop scheduled F-13-style so a bad frame can't kill it (`journey.js:490`).

## №2 — Uncapped devicePixelRatio

- **S1 — ABSENT.** Capped at 2 in both canvases: `const dpr = Math.min(window.devicePixelRatio || 1, 2)` (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:286`; `StarCompassModal.tsx:112`). Gemini's 3×/9× blowup does not exist in his build. (Whether a cap of 2 satisfies PIS-01 thermal budgets on mid-range phones is a device question, not this defect.)
- **S2 — NOT-APPLICABLE.** The engine owns no canvas and no renderer — by design the renderer "owns art, DOM, canvas, WebGL — the engine owns none of it" (`world-engine.js:459`). The DPR obligation transfers whole to the Wave-2 renderer seat (see binding requirements).
- **S3 — NOT-APPLICABLE.** No canvas anywhere; `pose()` returns render-neutral data (`avatars.js`, pose method) for a host to draw.
- **S4 — ABSENT.** The journey is DOM/SVG; its two canvases size their backing store at CSS pixels, never multiplied by DPR: `W = c.width = c.offsetWidth` (motes, `journey.js:534`) and `c.width = c.offsetWidth` (burst, in `burst()`). There is no DPR scaling to cap — the cost is softness, not fill-rate.

## №3 — Wall-snag vs sliding tangent response

- **S1 — ABSENT.** Collision is a closest-point push-out applied to the attempted position every frame: `resolveCollisions(tr.x + tr.vx * dt, tr.z + tr.vz * dt, TRAVELER_RADIUS)` (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:393-397`; solver at `worldGeometry.ts:117`, two passes for corners). Push-out along the contact normal preserves the tangential velocity component, so a diagonal approach slides along the AABB faces instead of snagging; `computeWaypoints` (`worldGeometry.ts`, from line ~210) additionally routes around the central table so long crossings never scrape it. Honest caveat: sliding is *emergent* from positional resolution, not an explicit tangent projection, and every specimen obstacle is an AABB — the behaviour is proven for boxes, which is all the specimen contains.
- **S2 — ABSENT at the model level.** `geometry.js` carries the same family, generalized: `resolvePoint` (`geometry.js:248`) — two-pass push-out, nearest-face escape from inside, circle + rect obstacles — and `route()` (`geometry.js:338`) plans waypoint paths around obstacles so a route-follower need never scrape a wall. Per-frame sliding *feel* is a property of a locomotion consumer, which does not exist yet; it is carried into the binding requirements rather than claimed here.
- **S3 — NOT-APPLICABLE.** The walker has no collision concept at all: `update()` steps straight at the target (avatars.js, update method). Nothing exists to snag on — and, symmetrically, nothing stops a body crossing furniture. That is deliberate layering (pathfinding/collision are the geometry layer's documented NOT-built), but it makes the wiring seat's pairing of walker + `route()`/`resolvePoint()` load-bearing (requirements).
- **S4 — NOT-APPLICABLE.** Prototype walkers move straight-line with no collision system (`makeWalker.update`, `journey.js:340-352`) — documented prototype scope; its spatial model has anchors and occlusion, not solids.

## №4 — Viewport-coupled coordinates

- **S1 — ABSENT.** All gameplay data lives in logical world space: `FLOOR_BOUNDS` x ∈ [−0.84, 0.84], z ∈ [0.14, 0.93] (`worldGeometry.ts:4-9`), obstacles in world units (`worldGeometry.ts:13-48`), door stand-points in world units (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:95, 107`). Pixels exist only inside `worldToScreen`/`screenToWorld`, fed by per-frame `ProjectionMetrics`. One render-time note, not a defect: `stageW = Math.max(width, 860)` (`worldGeometry.ts:73`) floors the *projected* stage width, so on a 375 px phone the floor is wider than the viewport and the camera offset (camX) does the framing — stored coordinates never move, so doors cannot misalign at 9:16; only the framing choice needs a design eye when a house renderer adopts the same floor.
- **S2 — ABSENT.** The scene schema is normalized end to end: x in stage-percent (exits at 3/97, rest positions ~40), depth 0..1, floor top/bottom as percents (`scenes.example.json`, every scene). `geometry.js` works in normalized geometry space and converts explicitly (`scenePointToWorld()`/`worldPointToScene()`, contract at `geometry.js:25-27`). The specimen's 860 floor survives only as projection config — `minStageW: 860` in `DEFAULT_PROJECTION` (`geometry.js:79`), applied inside `stageW(viewport)` (`geometry.js:99`) — never in stored data.
- **S3 — ABSENT.** Walker state is stage-percent x (clamped −16..116, off-stage exits included — `avatars.js:131-133`) and depth d ∈ [0, 1]. No pixel value exists anywhere in the module.
- **S4 — ABSENT.** Client pixels are converted to percent/depth at the event boundary and never stored: stage click → `const x = (e.clientX - r.left) / r.width * 100` and `const d = clamp((feetPct - f.top) / (f.bottom - f.top), 0, 1)` (`journey.js:659-662`). All scene data (SPATIAL) is percent + depth; the only px in rendering is walker height derived from stage height at draw time (`makeWalker.render`).

## №5 — Touch-input & browser gesture conflicts

- **S1 — ABSENT.** The canvas speaks Pointer Events — `onPointerDown`/`onPointerMove` (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:694-700`) — unifying mouse and touch, and carries `touch-none select-none` (Tailwind `touch-action: none`) at `ArchiveCanvas.tsx:700`, so the browser cannot steal the gesture for scroll or pull-to-refresh over the world. Tap-to-walk fires on pointerdown (no click delay); the design has no drag gestures, so no deadzone is needed.
- **S2 — NOT-APPLICABLE.** The engine binds no pointer or touch input at all; its only listener is `hashchange` (`world-engine.js`, bindHash). Input discipline belongs to the host renderer — carried to requirements.
- **S3 — NOT-APPLICABLE.** No DOM, no input; the host feeds `setTarget()`.
- **S4 — CONFIRMED-PRESENT (partial).** What works: tap-to-walk via stage click (`journey.js:655`), and the one drag surface — the parts scrubber — has a real 40 px swipe deadzone with passive listeners (`journey.js:800-809`). What is missing: **no `touch-action` and no `overscroll-behavior` anywhere in `journey.css`** (zero hits), and the journey is a scrolling document wrapped around a stage of `height: min(68svh, 620px)` (`.stage`, `journey.css:39-43`; `body` carries no overscroll rule, `journey.css:14`). A vertical drag that starts on the stage can therefore scroll the document or trigger pull-to-refresh on a phone instead of staying in the world. Hash routing restores the scene after a refresh, so recovery is decent — but the gesture conflict itself is real, and desktop verification never touches it.

## №6 — Teardown memory leaks

- **S1 — ABSENT for what exists; the multi-place half is NOT-APPLICABLE.** The component's effect cleanup cancels its rAF and removes all three window listeners (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:566-571`); the modal removes its keydown listener (`StarCompassModal.tsx:69`) and cancels its rAF (`StarCompassModal.tsx:181`). No `ResizeObserver` and no `setInterval` exist anywhere in the specimen source. But Gemini's scenario — "leaks the moment a visitor transitions between two places" — cannot occur here because the specimen *has* no second place: doors open panels inside the one hall. That is the already-recorded hard-coded-single-place limit, not a leak.
- **S2 — ABSENT.** The lifecycle is explicit and complete: `start()` is idempotent (`if (rafId !== null) return api;` — `world-engine.js:597`), `stop()` cancels the frame and nulls the id (`world-engine.js:605-608`), `unbindHash()` removes the hashchange listener (`world-engine.js:624-627`). Scene changes inside the engine never spawn loops — one master loop for the world's whole life — so there is nothing per-place to leak. One care point for hosts: `bindHash()` adds a listener per call, so a host that binds per mount must unbind per unmount (requirements).
- **S3 — NOT-APPLICABLE.** The module owns no rAF, listeners, observers or timers; it is a pure state machine advanced by host `update(dt)` calls. Even the companion's chuckle/gesture timing is arithmetic on an internal clock (`companion.js`, update), not `setTimeout`. Nothing exists to leak.
- **S4 — ABSENT.** Every loop is a boot-time singleton started exactly once: the engine loop (`journey.js:490-492`, kicked off at `journey.js:911`), the motes loop, the parallax chase loop. Route changes start none of them again. The one transient loop — the manifestation particle burst — self-terminates when its particles die and stops under reduced motion (`burst()`, alive flag). Manifestation `setTimeout` chains are guarded by a sequence token so a stale sequence aborts rather than firing into the next state (`journey.js:692, 701-710`). Population is rebuilt per scene with the host emptied and the walker list replaced (`buildPopulation`) — old walkers hold no listeners or timers and are garbage-collected. Window listeners are registered once for the page's lifetime, never per scene. (Nothing tears down on page unload — normal for a single-page prototype, and rAF auto-pauses in background tabs.)

## №7 — Reduced-motion handling + secondary dynamic CDN calls

- **S1 — CONFIRMED-PRESENT, both halves.** Reduced motion: the specimen's *entire* coverage is one CSS rule disabling the compass spin — `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { .compass-gentle-spin { animation: none; } }` (`index.css:133-137`). No JavaScript in the specimen reads `prefers-reduced-motion` (zero `matchMedia` hits across `src/`), so the world loop — traveler, owl, dust, ripples, door glows — animates at full rate for a reduced-motion visitor. CDN: `index.html:12-14` loads Google Fonts (two preconnects + the css2 stylesheet) — a real external dependency, the zero-call dent the examination already logged. No *further* dynamic calls hide in the bundles: zero `fetch(`/XHR/`new Image` hits in `src/`, and the `@import "tailwindcss"` at `index.css:1` is a build-time package import, not a network call.
- **S2 — CDN half ABSENT; reduced-motion half NOT-APPLICABLE at this layer.** No URLs, no fetch, no external imports (geometry.js imports only `./world-engine.js` siblings). The engine is a clock + data model and exposes no reduced-motion flag or query (zero hits in `world-engine.js`/`geometry.js`) — the obligation is knowingly carried by consumers (S3 and S4 both implement it). A renderer seat gets no engine-level help and must read the media query itself (requirements).
- **S3 — ABSENT.** Reduced motion is first-class state: `setReducedMotion` (`avatars.js:142`) makes `update()` place-never-glide while still firing arrival (`avatars.js:146-152`); the companion propagates it to its walker (`companion.js:89`), freezes its rig clock to a still (`companion.js:160`) and reports idle mode (`companion.js:174, 199`). No external references of any kind.
- **S4 — ABSENT, both halves.** Reduced motion is read once from `matchMedia` (`journey.js:29`) and gates: leg swing and body rock (`journey.js:358, 362`), population life (`journey.js:398`), glide-vs-place in the engine loop (`journey.js:495-507`), Solnik's bob (`journey.js:521`), parallax (`journey.js:537, 562`), scene transitions (`journey.js:623`), manifestation timing (`journey.js:700, 710`), the dust and burst loops (`journey.js:731` region), and scroll behaviour (`journey.js:792`); `journey.css:235-236` adds the CSS leg. CDN: `index.html` references only `journey.css` and a `data:,` favicon (`index.html:8-9`); zero external URLs in JS or CSS.

## What binds the Wave-2 wiring work — requirements, in priority order

1. **Touch discipline is the renderer's first law (from №5/S4, №5/S2).** The shipping reference proves the failure is real in our own code: no `touch-action`, no `overscroll-behavior`, a scrolling document around the stage. The Wave-2 host shell must set `touch-action: none` on the stage (or a deliberate pan scope) and `overscroll-behavior: none` on the shell, and tap-to-walk must ride pointerdown, as the specimen does (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:700`).
2. **Never feed S3 a raw clock (from №1/S3).** Walkers and the companion take dt on trust. Wiring must drive them exclusively from the S2 master clock (clamped at `world-engine.js:409`) — or the walker contract gains its own ceiling. A host that wires `update()` straight to its own rAF re-opens the teleport-arrival defect.
3. **Pair every walker with geometry, and prove sliding in integration (from №3/S3+S2).** S3 alone walks through furniture; `route()` + per-frame `resolvePoint()` (`geometry.js:338, 248`) are the collision system. Sliding is proven as a mechanism in S1/S2 but *nowhere as an integrated house behaviour* — the wiring seat's own exercise must include a diagonal wall-approach, not just detours.
4. **The renderer owns the DPR cap (from №2/S2).** Adopt the specimen's ceiling — `Math.min(devicePixelRatio || 1, 2)` (`ArchiveCanvas.tsx:286`) — as the floor requirement for any house canvas, and judge it against PIS-01 thermal behaviour on a real device, since neither engine nor avatar system will do it for you.
5. **The renderer owns reduced motion end-to-end (from №7/S2).** S2 offers no flag and no query. The host must read `prefers-reduced-motion` itself, call `setReducedMotion` on every S3 body, and freeze ambient loops — the S4 journey (`journey.js:29` + its gates) is the working pattern; the specimen's CSS-only coverage (№7/S1) is the anti-pattern.
6. **Use the engine lifecycle as written (from №6/S2).** One `start()`, `stop()` on exit from the world, and `bindHash()`/`unbindHash()` in matched pairs per mount. The engine protects against double-starts but not against a host that binds hash listeners repeatedly.
7. **Confirm the 860 px stage floor on phones before adopting the projection wholesale (from №4/S1+S2).** Stored coordinates are clean everywhere — but `minStageW: 860` (`geometry.js:79`, inherited from the specimen) makes the projected floor more than twice a 375 px viewport. The renderer seat must verify the resulting camera framing on a narrow screen is the intended design, not an inherited accident.
