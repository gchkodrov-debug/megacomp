# THE KOERNER STUDY — Chris Koerner, Richard Yu, and the Digital-Products Playbook
### Research seat report · 2026-11-26 · $0 spent, nothing bought, nothing signed up for

---

## THE VERDICT IN PLAIN WORDS (read this part only if you're tired)

1. The "$32M CEO" in that video title is **not Chris Koerner — it's his guest, Richard Yu.** The $32 million is Yu's own marketing claim, and nobody has ever seen a breakdown of it.
2. Koerner himself is real: real businesses, real exits, covered by real magazines. But his own digital product made **$23,000 on launch weekend — not millions.**
3. And he made that $23,000 by emailing a list of roughly **a quarter-million people** who already trusted him. He says it himself in the video: the list is his "unfair advantage."
4. His actual method is simple and honest: take material you already have, test two prices on a small slice of your list, show the price in the email, use the checkout with the least friction, then send to everyone.
5. The method transfers to us. **The numbers do not.** Same email to our audience today makes dollars, not thousands — because the machine that made it work is the audience, and we don't have one yet.
6. The biggest lesson for OneJobKits is uncomfortable: **we have 774 products and no list. He had one folder of old files and a huge list. He won.**
7. Second lesson: his $97 price beat his $47 price — more total money, better buyers, fewer refunds. That supports our high-ticket direction.
8. Third lesson: never hide the price. Hiding it cut his sales by more than half. And every extra click or account-signup at checkout cost him 30% of sales.
9. Yu's side of the video — AI-built products, AI avatar videos, selling other people's products for commission — is a funnel into Yu's paid program. The traffic part (the part that matters) is always "in the next video."
10. **Bottom line: copy Koerner's testing discipline and his list-first thinking. Ignore both men's headline numbers. Build the list; the products are already built.**

## 2 · THE METHOD, END TO END

### 2A · KOERNER'S OWN LAUNCH — "Plans of Grandeur" (Koerner Office Ep. #285, full transcript retrieved)

The setup: Meta banned his Facebook page; six figures of income vanished overnight. On a run the next morning he decided to launch a business **on camera, same day**. His words: *"Anytime anyone starts a business, you need to go to your unfair advantage… one of my unfair advantages is that I have a newsletter."*

**WHAT he sold:** A Google Drive folder of *already-existing, never-published* material from his old 1-on-1 creator consulting (he charged **$10,000/month** for that consulting): training videos, guides, spreadsheets, tracking sheets. **Zero new product was created.** Named "Plans of Grandeur." Sold as one-time, lifetime access, plus 12 live AMA calls. 10% of revenue donated publicly to the National Kidney Foundation.

**THE METHOD, step by step (his sequence, his words):**
1. **Randomize the list, split it, test before you blast.** He exported his Beehiiv list, randomized it, and ran small-sample A/B tests first — *"after a few more A/B tests, I'm going to know exactly what people need and then I'll send the right price with the right messaging to everyone."* About a quarter of the list got test emails before the full send.
2. **Price test — $47 vs $97** (identical email, only the Stripe link differed). Results in Section 4. **He chose $97** — slightly more total revenue after a week, and deliberately: *"people that are willing to invest… fewer support requests, fewer complaints, fewer refund requests. Generally speaking… better to sell a more expensive product."*
3. **Price-visibility test — show the price in the email vs hide it.** Showing the price won by more than 2-to-1. His stated lesson: **"Tell them the price in the email."**
4. **Checkout test — bare Stripe payment link vs Skool community checkout.** Stripe won by 30%. His diagnosis: Skool forced buyers to create an account first — *"I hate friction when it comes to trying to sell something."* Delivery stayed a plain Google Drive link after Stripe payment; the Skool community was back-filled afterward for the buyers.
5. **Email test — shorter email beat the long story email.**
6. **Then the full send** with the winning combination (short email, price shown, $97, Stripe link): sent 9:18 AM → **125 sales = $12,125 in 80 minutes.**
7. **Afterward:** price raised as promised (the pre-sale email had framed $97 as a discount off a $291 launch price), product moved into Skool, folded into his email automations as an evergreen earner — *"a few thousand per month pretty passively."*

**PLATFORM:** Beehiiv (list) → Stripe payment links (checkout) → Google Drive (delivery) → later Skool (community home). Not Gumroad, not Stan.

**TRAFFIC:** 100% his own newsletter. No ads, no affiliates, no SEO for this launch. The audience was built over ~2 years of daily short-form content feeding the list.

**FUNNEL SHAPE:** Free daily content (100M views/mo) → newsletter (owned list) → one $97 one-time product → community + AMAs → his higher-ticket rooms (TKOwners membership; Cofounders; his funds). The $97 product is the *front door*, not the business.

**FROM-ZERO REPEATABILITY:** He is honest about it on camera: *"Before you write this off and say, oh, this is easy for him, he has a big email list…"* — then argues small lists convert better per-person because of closeness. Both things are true: the *mechanism* (test → send) works at any size; the *revenue* scales linearly with list size × trust. His own test math: **$0.08–$0.27 of revenue per subscriber emailed.** A 500-person list running his exact playbook should expect roughly **$40–$135 per send**, not $12,125 in 80 minutes.

**Current state of the product:** "Plans of Grandeur" lives on Skool at **$97 one-time, 1,074 members** (Skool's public listing, crawled this week). If all members paid full price, that's ≈ $104K cumulative gross since Feb 2026 — consistent with a strong launch plus months of evergreen promotion to a growing list. He also now teaches the system as a paid **"$97 Digital Product Launch System" workshop with HighLevel** — where he is a *disclosed HighLevel affiliate*. The teaching is also a sales channel.

### 2B · RICHARD YU'S METHOD (from his own videos, as summarized by transcript services and The Opportunity Desk)

Yu's five steps (his "most valuable training" video):
1. **Validate demand before creating** — build a "demand map" from repeated questions, comments under high-performing posts, and products already selling. Sell the *outcome*, not the method.
2. **Package the offer** — hook, first line, cover, name, promise, price. Make two content types: *reach content* (broad, attracts) and *conversion content* (specific problem + clear next step).
3. **Deliver on the promise** — open with *Promise → Proof → Plan*; he says refunds come from promise/product mismatch, not product quality.
4. **Turn attention into buyers** — four paths, least to most personal: link in bio → reply to comments with link → comment-a-keyword DM flows → personal conversations.
5. **Watch four numbers weekly** — views, clicks/comments, conversations, sales — and fix the narrowest point. His illustration: 30,000 views → 1% click → 300 clicks → 5% buy → **15 sales**.

Yu's from-scratch build (his July 2026 video): pick one specific person with one specific problem → confirm people complain about it (Claude + web search, Reddit, Instagram comments) → **$27 PDF** (Claude drafts it, Canva cover) → **$197 mid-tier** (no-code app via Base44, or videos + templates) → **$2,000+ coaching** for buyers of the first two. **Or skip building:** promote someone else's product from allen.store at up to **90% commission**.

Yu's traffic system (his "$32.7M system" video): find proven products on allen.store/ClickBank paying 50–100% → build a storefront in Wix AI → copy scripts from top Instagram videos in the niche → regenerate them as **HeyGen AI-avatar videos** → post daily → link in bio.

**The catch, documented by The Opportunity Desk's review:** that video is "part one of a free four-part series," customer acquisition is deferred to part three, and the series ends with a "free bonus valued at over $1,000" — i.e., a funnel into his paid program (Impact Clients). The free content teaches product-building (the easy part) and sells the traffic part.

**Koerner (operator's view — AI as tool and as service, not as product):**
- In the launch itself, AI was backstage: he asked **Claude** to sanity-check the Stripe-vs-Skool decision from his A/B screenshots (Claude agreed, citing friction), used **ChatGPT** to shorten email copy, and had **Claude generate the product logo**. Service first, product later.

- His podcast platform amplifies guests selling *tiny* AI products — e.g., guest Ryan Dozer's "Claude Code Skills Stack" (packaged prompts/skills, non-technical builder): **$3,000+ passive in 45 days** (guest's claim). Koerner's framing: app-store-style micro-tools anyone can now build and sell.

**Yu (AI as the whole factory):**
- AI *writes* the product (Claude → PDF), AI *builds* the software tier (Base44 no-code app), AI *is* the traffic (HeyGen avatar videos re-performing proven scripts), AI *replaces* product creation entirely in the arbitrage variant (other people's products, 50–90% commissions).
- Note the asymmetry: Yu sells the *idea* of AI products inside a coaching funnel; Koerner uses AI inside businesses that already sell something.

## 5 · TRANSFER ANALYSIS — FOR ONEJOBKITS

**Our position, for the comparison:** ~774 products researched/built (394 live on Shopify/Gumroad, ~380 town-side), **$0 revenue to date**, pivoting to high-ticket tools ($100–$1,000), small owned audience (store list minimal, small Discord, small X), and a standing law: **no fake evidence, ever** — no invented testimonials, no simulated sales.

### What transfers cleanly (mechanism, not vibe)
1. **The test-before-you-blast protocol.** Randomize what audience you have, send two versions differing in ONE variable (price / price shown / checkout path), decide on revenue-per-recipient, then roll out the winner. This is pure method — it works with 300 people; it just answers slower. It is also the exact discipline our 774-product catalogue has never had: we have never run one controlled price test.
2. **Show the price, early, everywhere.** His single most lopsided result: hiding the price halved sales at equal clicks. Our listings and any future launch emails should state the price in the first screen — no "click to reveal," no DM-for-price.
3. **Checkout friction is a tax we can measure.** A forced account signup cost him 30%. Our rule: guest checkout, fewest fields, the buy button lands on the payment page — not on a community, a signup, or a "join first" wall.
4. **The product is already built — sell the folder.** His entire launch used zero new creation: existing material, repackaged, renamed, priced. Our bottleneck is identical in shape: 774 finished/near-finished products, zero launches run with this discipline. The scarce resource is distribution, not inventory.
5. **Higher price = better economics AND better customers.** $97 beat $47 on total revenue after the deliberation lag, with (his reasoning) fewer refunds and support tickets. This is real evidence *for* our high-ticket pivot — at the $47→$97 step. It does not prove $995 converts like $97; it proves "cheapest" is not the safe default we might assume.
6. **Own the list or the platform owns you.** The whole episode exists because Meta deleted six figures of his income overnight. His hedge was an owned email list. Every surface we run (store, Discord, X, the PROVADIA town) currently rents its audience.
7. **Yu's demand map as a pruning tool.** Rank products by *external* demand evidence — repeated buyer questions, comments under top content in the niche, competitors actually selling the same thing — before spending one hour promoting them. Yu's name for building without it: "posting into the void." That is a precise description of publishing 774 products with no demand check per product.
8. **Yu's four-number weekly funnel** (views → clicks → conversations → sales; fix the narrowest point). Applied honestly to us today, it names our bottleneck in one line: it is not product quality and not checkout — it's the top, traffic and conversations ≈ 0.

### What does NOT transfer, and why
1. **The launch revenue.** $12,125 in 80 minutes = ~250K warm subscribers × personal trust × $0.08–$0.27/subscriber. Our list is a rounding error next to his. Same playbook, our scale: tens of dollars per send at first. Anyone who budgets Koerner outcomes from a Koerner method without a Koerner list is doing the exact math error this study exists to prevent.
2. **Personal-brand trust as the conversion engine.** His email sells *Chris* — his ban story, his kidney donation, his psychiatrist anecdote, a decade of faces-on-camera content. OneJobKits has no equivalent public person. Our offers must convert on product proof (demos, samples, guarantees we can honor), which is slower and is exactly why our no-fake-evidence law makes the first proof the hardest.
3. **His price tier.** His proof lives at $47–$97 — impulse-to-light-consideration money, decided in days. Our pivot tier ($495/$995 systems) is a considered business purchase. His conversion rates (0.3–0.8% of email openers) will not survive contact with 5–10× prices; at high ticket, the email's job changes from "close now" to "start a conversation" — closer to Yu's path #4 than to Koerner's blast.
4. **Yu's arbitrage + AI-avatar traffic.** Promoting strangers' products at 90% commission with AI faces re-reading copied scripts (a) collides head-on with our no-fake-evidence law, (b) is the content class platforms actively suppress (The Opportunity Desk flags this; Koerner himself got banned by Meta in an adjacent play), and (c) builds no asset we'd own.
5. **His content machine.** 3 videos/day, ~100M views/month, a paid content team, $6/video pipeline, 715K→11.9M podcast downloads. Copying the *output* without the machine is not a plan; the transferable piece is only the ratio discipline (track views→subscribers; CTA every time).
6. **Meta/Facebook as a channel.** His origin story is a ban. Our own store Instagram is already permanently disabled by Meta. Any Koerner-style Facebook play starts from a hole we're already in.

### The 3 most actionable lessons, in order
1. **Run one Koerner-protocol launch this week — on ONE flagship product, to whatever audience exists.** Pick a single finished flagship kit. Write the short email/post. A/B exactly one variable (e.g., $197 vs $297) across two halves of every channel we have (store list, Discord, X, Gumroad followers). Price shown in the message. Link straight to the lowest-friction checkout. Measure revenue per recipient and total sales; publish the result internally whatever it is. This converts 774 silent products into our first real market data point — the thing both men agree everything else depends on.
2. **Make email capture the #1 build priority — ahead of any new product.** A free flagship-grade kit as the lead magnet (we already run a giveaway engine), one capture form on every surface — store, town doors, Discord, X bio. Koerner's career survived a platform execution because of his list; right now, one bad platform decision erases our entire reach. Target: the list becomes the launch channel every future product uses, so each launch is bigger than the last — his flywheel, at our scale.
3. **Prune 774 → a fighting five, using the demand map.** Score every catalogue product on external demand evidence (search demand, competitor sales proof, real questions in real communities). Promote only the top handful with concentrated effort; park the rest without guilt. 774 products with zero traffic each is not a catalogue, it's the "post cliff" — Yu's #1 failure pattern, documented across 3,000 sellers he tracked. Five products with traffic beat 774 with none, and the five generate the proof (real sales, real buyers) that high-ticket offers need.

### Warning signs (read before admiring either man)
- **The headline number belongs to the guest and is unverifiable.** $32M/$32.7M shifts between Yu's videos, has no revenue/profit/coaching breakdown, and sits atop a funnel: free video series → deferred traffic lesson → paid program. This is the classic guru shape: teach the easy 20% free, sell the hard 80%.
- **Survivorship, narrated by the survivor.** Koerner's launch video is content *about* the product that *sells* the product — the video is itself the funnel, and its happy numbers are the ad. His 80+ businesses include failures he waves past; the one business profiled in depth by independent media (Texas Snax) is real but smaller than his telling ($250–300K/mo per *The Hustle* vs. his $400K+).
- **Koerner's receipts are screen-shared but self-reported.** The Ep. #285 Stripe walkthrough is the strongest class of self-report — and still unaudited, on his account, in a video that monetizes the story. Grade: credible, not verified.
- **The teacher is also a salesman.** Koerner teaches the $97 system through a paid HighLevel workshop where he's a disclosed affiliate; his newsletter runs on (and promotes) Beehiiv; Yu promotes allen.store inside his "free" training. Every recommendation in both men's content has a commission or a funnel behind it.
- **Refund/support reality is asserted, not shown.** Koerner's "$97 buyers refund less" is reasoning, not data he displayed. Yu's student figures are gross sales, cherry-picked, with his own "atypical" caveats doing legal work.
- **Platform risk is the silent co-author.** Koerner's story starts with a ban; Yu's avatar-farming runs toward the same wall. Any playbook that begins "first, get a platform to lend you an audience" ends the way both men's stories keep almost ending.
