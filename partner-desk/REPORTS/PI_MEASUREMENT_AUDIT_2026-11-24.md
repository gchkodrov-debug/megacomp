# PI MEASUREMENT AUDIT — THE ACTIVITY FIGURES
## Were incompatible figures compared?

**Commissioned:** 2026-11-24 00:36 CT, under ChatGPT's challenge to the captain's Pi case study (`PI_NETWORK_CASE_STUDY_2026-11-24.md`), carried and endorsed by the founder. **The question, exactly:** the study set Pi's claimed 60M+ "engaged" against an estimated ~20–32K daily active users. ChatGPT: active blockchain addresses, active application users and paying customers are *different measurements* — were incompatible figures compared? **Method:** Relay Law — two independent research seats (A: origin trace · B: primary sources) + the captain's own first-hand verification + a different-family critic seat whose findings changed this audit's conclusions (Relay Record, §7). Public sources only, $0, no signups. Pi's own numbers are labeled **SELF-REPORTED** wherever they appear. **Scope fence (founder's order):** no spending, registration, submission or deployment was authorized or performed.

## §0 · THE VERDICT IN ONE PAGE

**Yes — as stated, the comparison mixes incompatible figures. The direction survives the audit; the precision does not.**

1. **The two sides measure different things.** "60M+ engaged" is a stock figure with **no published definition** — no activity window, no qualifying action, no deduplication rule (Seat B, exhaustive search of Core Team posts). The daily figures are *flows* of on-chain accounts initiating operations. A stock of unknown definition set against a daily on-chain flow is not an activity rate, and must never be quoted as one.
2. **The "~20–32K daily" range is an editorial splice, not a measurement.** Traced to origin (§1): the 20K floor is a CCN average over ExplorePi data from the *enclosed-mainnet era* (published Oct 2024, updated Jan 2025; ExplorePi's own definition of "active wallet" has never been published); the 32K ceiling is, probably, a CoinLaunch single-day ExplorePi snapshot (~Sep 2025). No source before AInvest (mid-2026) publishes "20,000–32,000" as a range, and AInvest states it with no method, no citation — and a percentage (0.1–0.3%) that does not match its own stated denominator (§1.3).
3. **The study's "~14K/day" is an artefact, and this audit withdraws it.** It is 27,389 ÷ 2 — the Pi Logistics census (the one genuinely primary source in this field) never published a daily figure. Halving a 48-hour distinct count assumes zero day-to-day overlap. The census supports only a bound on the *average* daily count of successful-operation initiators across its two days: **between ~13,700 and ~27,400** (§2.2).
4. **What the best evidence actually supports:** in a full 48-hour census of Pi's public ledger (29–31 Oct 2026), **27,389 distinct accounts initiated at least one operation in a successful transaction** — an average-daily equivalent of ~13.7K–27.4K, i.e. **~0.08–0.17% of the migrated base** (16,568,774, snapshot 5 Mar 2026) initiating on-chain operations on an average day. That is a *successful on-chain initiating-account* measure. It excludes accounts whose only activity sat inside failed transactions (a further 27,774 accounts appear among the failures; the union is unmeasured), excludes receiving-only accounts, and — per the census author — excludes all exchange and in-app activity that never reaches the ledger. **Pi publishes no app DAU/MAU and no commerce figure at all** (§3, §4), so no honest "daily active users" or "paying customers" number for Pi exists in public.
5. **The founder's instinct is the audit's conclusion:** the lesson is not "Pi has 25,000 users." The lesson is that Pi's *claimed* number cannot be compared to *any* measured number because Pi never defined it — and that a house which publishes definitions with its numbers can never be caught this way (§6).

## §1 · ORIGIN TRACE — WHERE "20,000–32,000 DAILY ACTIVE WALLETS" ACTUALLY COMES FROM

### 1.1 The citing source: AInvest (where the case study got the range)

- **Text (verified first-hand by the captain, 2026-11-24):** "Daily active wallets are estimated at only 20,000 to 32,000, highlighting a conversion rate of just 0.1% to 0.3% among the 35+ million registered users." — AInvest, *"Pi Network Faces Supply Overhang and Low On-Chain Activity Despite Ecosystem Growth,"* mid-2026 (URL slug `2606`; page prices PI "near $0.125 in mid-2026"; crawled 2026-09-01). https://www.ainvest.com/news/pi-network-faces-supply-overhang-chain-activity-ecosystem-growth-2606/
- **Method: none.** No citation, no explorer named, no window, no definition of "active," no derivation of either bound, no author byline or publication date displayed. **Grade: TERTIARY.** The case study called it "a single secondary estimate, methodology unstated" — if anything, generous.

### 1.2 The 20,000 floor — origin: CCN / ExplorePi (January 2025)

- **Text:** "On average, only about 20,000 wallets are active each day," with recorded peaks of 40,500 (Jul 2022), 35,200 (Jun 2024) and 42,136 (Jan 2025). — CCN, *"Pi Network: Why Your Coins May Be Worthless,"* author credited: Dr. Toghrul Aliyev, Head of Research, CCN. Published 2024-10-01; updated 2025-01-15 (the active-wallet passage is from the January 2025 update). https://www.ccn.com/analysis/crypto/pi-network-legit-scam-data-harvest-ad-network/
- **Data:** ExplorePi's published daily-active-wallet series (Figure 2 credited "CCN, Artemis Terminal, ExplorePi"), over a base of 9.11M wallets (Jan 2025). **ExplorePi's definition of an "active wallet" was not found in any source searched** — sender, any-operation party, balance-change, and day-boundary are all unstated. The data era is almost entirely the **enclosed, firewalled Mainnet**, before Open Network (20 Feb 2025).
- **The author's own caveats, dropped by every later republication:** the explorers "only track Mainnet activity, and not everyone has migrated"; Mainnet was not yet fully launched; and 0.22% daily-active "isn't necessarily poor in this context" (his Ethereum comparison: 0.12%).
- **Grade: SECONDARY · method UNKNOWN** (soundness rests entirely on ExplorePi's unpublished definition).

### 1.3 The 32,000 ceiling — probable origin: CoinLaunch / ExplorePi snapshot (~Sep 2025)

- **Text:** "Only 32,000 wallets demonstrate daily activity," attributed in-text to ExplorePi and illustrated with an ExplorePi dashboard screenshot. — CoinLaunch, *"Pi Coin Price Prediction 2025–2030,"* published 2025-09-13, updated 2026-06-10. https://coinlaunch.space/blog/pi-coin-price-prediction/
- **Character:** no snapshot date, no averaging window, no definition — it reads as a **single-day/current reading** off the dashboard, not an average. **Grade: TERTIARY · method UNKNOWN.** The trace is graded **probable**, not proven (critic §7): no pre-AInvest publication of "20,000–32,000" *as a range* was found, which is what makes the splice reconstruction the best-supported account — AInvest (or an unrecovered intermediary) joined CCN's *average* to CoinLaunch's *snapshot* and presented a mean and a one-day reading, from two different eras, as one confidence range.
- **A further defect inside AInvest itself (captain's arithmetic):** its stated "0.1% to 0.3%" does not follow from its own numbers. 20,000 / 35M = 0.057% and 32,000 / 35M = 0.091% — the percentage range matches *neither* bound against its stated "35+ million registered users" denominator. (Against the migrated count the same article cites, 16.7M, the figures give 0.12–0.19%.) The article carries three denominators — 35M+ registered, 18.1M KYC, 16.7M migrated — and its headline percentage is consistent with none of them as printed.
- **Propagation, for the record:** the spliced range is now repeated verbatim downstream (e.g. WEEX's 2026 beginner's guide attributes it onward to CoinStats). A number with no method acquires authority purely by repetition — the exact failure this audit exists to catch.

### 1.4 What the splice does to the comparison

Two statistics of different kinds (a multi-year *average* vs a *single-day reading*), from two different network eras (enclosed vs open Mainnet), under a definition nobody has published, joined into a range and then set against a claimed stock figure from a third, undefined definition. **Each layer alone would fail a measurement audit; stacked, the "20–32K" range cannot be quoted as a measurement at all** — only as a claim about what third parties have asserted.

## §2 · THE PRIMARY SOURCE — THE PI LOGISTICS CENSUS, AND WHAT IT DOES AND DOES NOT SAY

### 2.1 The census

- **Source:** Pi Logistics (Pi Logistics Ltd; @pillghana), *"Two networks, 48 hours each,"* published 31 Oct 2026. https://pillgh.com/blog/two-networks-48-hours/ (method companion, 26 Oct 2026: https://pillgh.com/blog/one-approval-in-96376/). **Grade: PRIMARY · method SOUND for what it measures** — the strongest source in this entire field.
- **Method:** full-window enumeration (not a sample) of Pi's public Mainnet Horizon API — *all* transactions, and all operations in successful transactions, **04:21 UTC 29 Oct → 04:21 UTC 31 Oct 2026**; 34,561 ledgers; totals checked against every ledger header. Read-only; author unaffiliated with Pi Network (disclosed; also discloses running a Testnet arbitrage bot and its own test tokens).
- **Findings:** 776,704 transactions attempted; 511,890 failed (**65.9%**; 98% of failures were claimable-balance claims — migration/unlock mechanics, not commerce); 540,008 operations inside successful transactions; **27,389 distinct accounts started at least one operation**; 39.35M Pi moved in payments to others (all payment operations, untagged — exchange/service/peer flows included, **not** identified commerce); 3,967 new accounts created, funded by just 2 accounts.
- **The author's own caveats (material, and adopted by this audit):** (1) activity inside exchanges and apps "never reaches the ledger as a separate transaction"; (2) concentration — the ten busiest accounts made 52% of all successful operations, a flow pattern belonging to exchanges or large services; (3) "any figure for Pi Mainnet activity that does not separate failures overstates real use by about three times" (arithmetic checks: 1/(1−0.659) ≈ 2.93); (4) the failed-claim pattern — busiest failing accounts failed 157–180 times each, active in 45+ of the 48 hours — "is the pattern of software retrying on a timer, not of people pressing a button."

### 2.2 The "~14K/day" — withdrawn

The case study's §1.1 derived "~14K/day" by halving 27,389, and flagged at the time that it "does not follow arithmetically from the 48-hour census." This audit completes that flag: **the figure is withdrawn as a measurement.** Halving assumes zero accounts were active on both days. The census supports exactly one statement: the *average* daily count of distinct successful-operation initiators across those two days lies between **13,695** (if the two days' sets were disjoint) and **27,389** (if identical). It bounds no individual day. No evidence in hand measures the day-to-day overlap, and this audit makes no claim about where inside the interval the truth sits (the critic struck an earlier draft's "upper half" inference as speculation — the bot-retry evidence concerns the *failed*-claim account set, not the successful initiators, and operation concentration is not account overlap).

### 2.3 The failed-transaction set — the census's second, unmerged number

Failures were spread across **27,774 distinct accounts — a larger set than the 27,389** — and operations *inside* failed transactions were not read (failures were characterised from transaction records: account, time, result code). Therefore the count of distinct accounts that *attempted* to initiate anything in the window is the union of the two sets: unmeasured, bounded only within **[27,774, 55,163]**. Every figure in this audit derived from 27,389 is therefore labeled, precisely, **successful-operation initiators** — never "active accounts" without the qualifier.

### 2.4 The other derivations — and an unreconciled contradiction

- **Web3Wagmi (17 Aug 2026):** a direct Horizon sample (200 ledgers, 17.2 minutes, 338 successful transactions) extrapolated to **~28,325 successful transactions/day** — a transactions figure, not users; a 17-minute window makes it shaky as a daily estimate. https://blog.web3wagmi.com/pi-network-guide
- **The contradiction, recorded not resolved:** the census implies (776,704 − 511,890) / 2 ≈ **132,407 successful transactions/day** (Oct 2026) — ~4.7× the Web3Wagmi extrapolation from August. Sample bias, seasonality, or definitional difference could each explain it; no source in hand adjudicates. Any future use of either figure must carry this discrepancy.
- **Community monitor (@jojo102102, ~Nov 2026):** ~274,350 tx/day claimed, ~62% payment operations (a second repost of the same monitor says 78%); no published method; sits between the census's successful-only (~132K/day) and attempted (~388K/day) rates and is irreconcilable with either without its method. **Not used** — the case study was right to exclude it.
- **Full-chain census (genesis → 1 Nov 2026):** 18,315,239 accounts ever created, from a complete scan of Horizon `signer_created` effects (18,444,110 events). **PRIMARY · SOUND** — for total accounts; it says nothing about daily activity. https://tysseo7-meru.github.io/pi-signer-created-report/signer_created_report_en.html
- **No explorer dashboard exists to appeal to:** neither Pi's official block explorer nor PiScan publishes a daily-active or transactions-per-day aggregate (PiScan itself has been offline since ~7 Sep 2026; its page today returns a maintenance notice). Aggregates exist only because independents crawl Horizon themselves — which is why this field is populated by ad-hoc censuses of varying quality.

## §3 · PRIMARY SOURCES — WHAT PI ITSELF PUBLISHES (observed 2026-11-24)

- **Pi's Horizon API is live and genuinely public** (captain-verified first-hand 2026-11-24 06:39 UTC: `api.mainnet.minepi.com`, network passphrase "Pi Network", protocol 28, latest ledger 29,913,813 closed 2026-11-24T06:39:39Z; per-ledger successful/failed transaction counts are exposed). Anyone *can compute* activity aggregates from it. **Pi does not publish the computed aggregates itself** — no daily-transactions series, no daily-active-address figure, no payments-volume total appears on the official explorer or API. Every daily figure in circulation is an independent or community derivation.
- **"Engaged" is undefined.** Across the Core Team posts using it (summit announcement, 15 Oct 2026: "more than 60 million Engaged Pioneers"; repeated 17 Nov 2026), no timeframe, activity threshold, deduplication or bot-exclusion rule is given anywhere. The figure has not moved since late 2024 while KYC and migration counts grew beneath it — consistent with a cumulative-style milestone rather than a live activity metric, but this audit records that as an *inference from behaviour of the number*, not a definition: the only definitional evidence on record is the Core Team spokesperson's statement to Cointelegraph (Feb 2025) that the 60M is "actually much lower than the total number of registered users" — i.e. it is *some* subset of registrations, by criteria never published.
- **KYC is unreconciled against itself.** 19M "identity-verified" at Open Network launch (20 Feb 2025) vs 18.1M "fully KYC'd" (first reported Apr 2026, unchanged since). Whether the labels differ in substance is never explained. This audit therefore does **not** use KYC as a ratio denominator, and says why rather than omitting it silently.
- **App activity: NO PUBLIC FIGURE.** No Pi-published, store-published or independently audited DAU/MAU exists for the Pi app or Pi Browser (corroborated in print: CCN — "Pi does not currently publish a standardized historical series showing monthly active users, daily active addresses, or monthly Mainnet transaction volumes"). What exists is downloads: Pi app 100M+ Google Play tier (~140M lifetime installs incl. re-installs, third-party panel estimate); Pi Browser 50M+. Downloads are not usage. **What would be needed:** SocialChain / Pi Community Company internal analytics (Play Console / App Store Connect / Firebase) with a stated DAU/MAU definition and window, or a labeled third-party panel estimate. Neither is publicly available.
- **Commerce: NO PUBLIC FIGURE.** PiFest 2025's official recap (1 Apr 2025) reports 125,000+ *registered* sellers, 58,000 *"active" sellers* (threshold undefined — the official text does not say "transacting"; secondary coverage that re-words it that way goes beyond the source), 1.8M Pioneers *used Map of Pi* (browsing is not buying), 45,000+ reviews (not verified purchases). No buyer count, no completed-purchase count, no commerce volume (Pi or fiat) has ever been published for PiFest, Map of Pi, or the network, for any period. The one published Pi-denominated activity at scale — the .pi domains auction (>57,000 unique bidders, >3M Pi in escrowed bids) — is escrowed commitments, not completed commerce. The census's 39.35M Pi/48h in payment operations cannot be promoted to commerce either: payments are untagged.

## §5 · THE VERDICT ON THE COMPARISON

**Question:** is "60M claimed vs ~20–32K daily" a fair juxtaposition, an unfair one, or fair-with-stated-caveats?

**Verdict: UNFAIR as a measurement; FAIR-WITH-STATED-CAVEATS as a contrast — and the specific numbers in it must both be corrected before any use.**

- *Unfair as a measurement,* for three independent reasons: (i) the numerator-side claim is undefined, so no ratio against it means anything; (ii) the daily range is a splice of an average and a snapshot from different eras under an unpublished definition (§1) — it is not a measurement of anything; (iii) the units differ on both sides simultaneously (claimed persons-ever vs measured accounts-per-day), so the juxtaposition as printed performs exactly the blending ChatGPT challenged.
- *Fair-with-stated-caveats as a contrast,* because the underlying phenomenon is real and independently supported: Pi claims a reach in the tens of millions, and the best primary evidence (a full-ledger census, not an estimate) finds the on-chain initiating population in the **low tens of thousands per day**. The gap between claimed reach and measured on-chain activity is roughly **three orders of magnitude** under any defensible pairing of the table's rows. That contrast — *claimed reach vs measured activity, each labeled as what it is* — is honest. A percentage "activity rate" computed across the two is not.

### 5.1 The honest ratio the evidence supports

Using the hardest denominator (migrated, row 4) and the primary numerator (row 6), with all caveats riding:

- Average-daily successful-operation initiators ÷ migrated accounts: 13,695 / 16,568,774 = **0.083%** to 27,389 / 16,568,774 = **0.165%** → **~0.08–0.17%**.
- Against accounts-ever (row 5, same unit, fresher): **~0.07–0.15%**.
- Against the claimed 60M+: the ratio can only be stated as a ceiling — **≤ ~0.05%** — because the denominator is a floor ("60M+") and, per §5 above, the pairing itself is a contrast, not a rate.
- Caveats that ride with every use: the numerator is *successful initiators only* (failed-only initiators, receivers, exchange-internal and in-app activity excluded — the true "touched the chain that day" population is larger by an unmeasured amount); the migrated denominator is a March 2026 snapshot against an October 2026 numerator (migration growth since would lower the ratio by an unknown amount); accounts ≠ persons in both directions.
- **Name:** this is a *successful on-chain initiating-account ratio*. This audit rejects calling it an "activity ratio" — that is the same overstatement, in miniature, that the audit was commissioned to catch.

### 5.2 The sentences

**PROVADIA may safely use:**
> "Pi claims more than 60 million 'engaged' users — a figure Pi has never defined. In the only full-ledger census available (48 hours, late October 2026), 27,389 distinct accounts initiated an operation in a successful transaction — on the order of one-tenth of one percent of Pi's migrated base (0.08–0.17% on the census bounds) touching the chain on an average day, before counting activity that never reaches the ledger at all."

**PROVADIA may never use:**
> "Only 20,000–32,000 of Pi's 60 million users are active" — it blends an undefined cumulative-style claim with a spliced, unsourced daily range, mislabels accounts as users on both sides, and states as a rate what is only a contrast. (The withdrawn "~14K/day" and any "Pi has ~25,000 users" formulation are banned by the same rule.)

**Required correction to the case study:** §0 and §1.1 of `PI_NETWORK_CASE_STUDY_2026-11-24.md` should be amended to (a) replace "~20–32K daily active wallets (a single secondary estimate…)" with the origin-traced account in §1 of this audit, (b) delete the "~14K/day" implication in favour of the [13,695 – 27,389] average-daily bound on successful initiators, and (c) carry the successful-only and unreconciled-transactions caveats wherever the census is cited. The study's *direction* — activity is a tiny fraction of the claimed base — is confirmed by this audit; its *precision* was borrowed, as it itself flagged.

## §6 · THE TRANSFERABLE RULE — SO NOBODY CAN DO THIS TO US

ChatGPT's standard, adopted as the audit's frame: **for PROVADIA, 10,000 genuine recurring customers outrank one million inactive accounts.** The rules below are stated as *candidate house rules for the reconciliation* (FVR-27 stays PROPOSED; nothing here is founded until the three seats reconcile and the founder rules):

- **R-1 · No number without its definition attached.** Every figure PROVADIA publishes — citizens, customers, transactions, volume — carries, in the same breath and the same visual weight: what counts, over what window, counted from what source, as of what date. A number whose definition cannot be printed beside it is not published.
- **R-2 · Stocks never pose as flows.** Cumulative-ever counts (registered, migrated, accounts created) are labeled cumulative, forever. They are never placed beside a daily or monthly figure in a way that lets a reader compute an "activity rate" we have not ourselves computed and defined.
- **R-3 · The funnel is published whole or not at all.** Registered · verified · active (defined) · paying — if we publish one stage, we publish the stages adjacent to it with the same definitions, so the conversion is visible rather than implied.
- **R-4 · On-chain counts and app counts live in separate columns, permanently.** Ledger-derived figures are labeled with their inclusion rules (successful only? initiators only? failures separated?) and are never renamed "users." App figures are labeled with their analytics source and definition. The two are never added, averaged or interchanged.
- **R-5 · Citizens and customers are never blended.** A citizen count is a community fact; a customer count is a commercial fact (a completed, paid transaction, refunds treated by a stated rule). No sentence, chart or pitch may spend one as if it were the other. Honors/achievement counts are recognition facts and are non-monetary (founder's standing fence, endorsed by all three seats).
- **R-6 · Self-reported is a label, not a shame — but it is always a label.** Our own numbers are marked as ours, with method, until an independent party can reproduce them from public data. Where our evidence is anchored (Day-Book root, Memo anchor), the verification recipe is published with the number.
- **R-7 · Failed and mechanical activity is separated before publication.** Any activity figure we publish states its treatment of failures, retries, bots and our own infrastructure accounts — before someone else's census does it for us. (Pi's 65.9% failure share, and its bot-retry signature, were discoverable by any stranger with two days and a public API. Assume the same of us.)
- **R-8 · When a number stops moving, say why.** A headline figure that sits unchanged while its funnel grows beneath it reads as a milestone posing as a metric. Our published figures carry "as of" dates, and a figure we can no longer stand behind is retired in public, with the reason — the correction is part of the record, as this audit is part of Pi's.
