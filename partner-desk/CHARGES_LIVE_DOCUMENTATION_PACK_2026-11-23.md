### A change in the town's terms — [DATE — set when charging is cleared]

The town's printed terms have called this service **free** — that word stands in the Mint's standing specification to this day. From **[DATE]**, the word changes: the Mint pilot is a charged service — **$50 per completed launch**, the price that has stood printed on this listing while checkout stayed closed. Now it becomes payable.

Read what "completed" means, because the charge hangs on it: the fee is taken **only when the launch is complete** — at the handover, when your coin is launched, your record is written, and you can run your coin unaided. Not before. There is no charge for a ticket, no charge for your number in the queue, and no charge if the work does not reach the handover — including if we stop it ourselves.

**What has not changed:**

- **The first assessment is still free** — including the honest question at the heart of the Mint: should this be a coin at all? A *no* there is still the service working, and it still costs nothing.
- **The Recovery Desk is still free.** **The Keeper's public answers are still free** — the Keeper himself is offline today, and until his counter opens, the town's support email is the working door for those answers.
- **Tickets are still issued free**, on a complete signed package.
- You still pay the chain's own creation costs from your own wallet, directly to the chain, as you always have. The town never touches that money.
- You still keep **100% of your coin's creator fees, forever.**

**How paying works, when it opens:** card, PayPal, or crypto, at the listing's own buy area. Pay in PROVADIA and the pilot is **$40 instead of $50** — the amount in coin is quoted exactly, with its rate source and expiry shown, before you pay. Of every fee, the payment costs come off the top first; of what remains, **80% goes to the founder and 20% to the website and the community**, accounted in the open. The town's refund policy stands beside this notice wherever the fee is charged.

**And the gate:** this notice does not open anything by itself. The intake gate opens only when the town says so here — after this notice has stood and the whole buying road, from this page to the handover, has been rehearsed and proven in testing. Until then, checkout stays closed and nothing can be bought.

— The Town

## PART C — REHEARSAL-VALIDATION CHECKLIST (sequence step 2)

**Purpose.** Before a real customer walks the purchase-to-delivery road, the whole road is walked in an **authorized test environment**, on **test rails**, under the **house test identity** — never with a real customer's money, never on the live till. Each link below is named, walked, and evidenced; a link passes only on a filed test record, and the road is proven only when every link has one. This checklist is the rehearsal validation the Q2 ruling requires; running it requires the founder's word and the step-2 authorization, neither of which this pack grants. **Terms used exactly:** *the test buyer* is the person walking the road in the test (in the service's own language, a test founder — the coin's founder in the rehearsal); *the town's founder* is the decision-maker of link 13. The two are never both called "the founder" in one link (corrected at the critic's Finding 4).

**The road, link by link:**

1. **Arrival** — a test buyer reaches the Mint listing by a direct product link (not through the homepage) and lands on the listing itself. *Pass: the listing is the first thing seen; no gate, queue, or homepage intercepts him.*
2. **Comprehension** — the listing answers the five questions for a stranger (Listing Standard §9, lines 1–5), including the §5.4 status block in its live form and the refund policy standing beside the price. *Pass: the filed five-question stranger test, run against the test listing.*
3. **The till, rail by rail** — hosted checkout in test mode on **all three rails** (card · PayPal · crypto), each carried to the thank-you page on our own site. *Pass: one completed test transaction per rail, receipted, with the disabled-state language correctly replaced only in the test environment.*
4. **The PROVADIA quote** — the fixed, time-limited checkout quote: rate source displayed, exact token amount, validity window, network costs stated, and the three edge behaviours walked — payment inside the window · payment after expiry · payment of a different amount. *Pass: each behaviour does what the listing's printed words (Q6) say it does.*
5. **Behind the counter** — conversion of test proceeds into PROVADIA (at receipt and in the disclosed daily batch — the designed mechanic of Listing Standard §5.1 and THE WHOLE HOUSE §6), and the **80/20 split on net receipts**: provider/network costs off the top first, the remainder divided, the No-Loss Rule checked arithmetically on every test transaction — **including the refund links of link 10**, whose stranded-cost behaviour the report must state expressly (see link 10) — so no test transaction ends with the house in the red unexplained. The discounted ($40) case is walked as well as the full-price case. *Pass: the test reconciliation balances, in the open format the live one will use.*
6. **The ticket road** — package received → ticket issued (free) → queue position shown → the call → the 72-hour response window, walked in test time: response inside the window · silence to EXPIRED/LAPSED · the town missing its own slot (the test buyer keeps priority). *Pass: every state transition receipted in the test Day-Book in the specification's state language.*
7. **The build road** — the eight steps in order (Conversation → Honest Question → Rehearsal → Covenant → Mark → Launch → Record → Handover), on testnet/test materials only, the **test buyer's own test wallet** signing every step itself. *Pass: no step performed by the town's hands that the service says the buyer's hands perform; custody never moves.*
8. **The refusal and stop drills** — a scope-check refusal (explained in writing, receipted, number kept) · a WITHDRAWN · a town STOPPED build (receipted as the town's act). *Pass: each drill produces the exact record and wording the standard and the refund policy promise — and, the fee being completion-only, no drill leaves a fee to return.*
9. **Delivery and the handover test** — registry entry with birth facts · House Passport issued with its four sections · source exports handed over · the test buyer operates unaided. *Pass: completion is declared only when the handover test of §6.3 is met — and the fee's test charge fires at exactly that moment, not before (Q12).*
10. **The refund paths** — a mistaken test charge on each rail, returned under the draft policy's §5 mechanics (same asset, same rail, inside the timeline); a duplicate payment; a payment against an expired quote. *Pass: every test return executes the policy's stated mechanics exactly — same asset, same rail, the §8.2 unwinding of the split balances — and the rehearsal report states expressly how the policy's §5.3 allocation behaves on a live transaction, where real provider/network costs are consumed and cannot all be recovered: who bears them in each case (town error · buyer error), and whether the founder has approved the §5.3(a) bounded amendment to the No-Loss Rule or the stated fallback applies. (In the test environment no real costs exist, so test returns restore in full by construction; the live behaviour is a documented founder question carried by the policy, not a rehearsal discovery — this link's wording was conformed to the policy at the critic's Finding 5.)*
11. **Support doors** — the support email answered end to end · the Keeper's status stated exactly as §7.3 requires while he is OFFLINE (no test may present him as live) · the 30-day window's start recorded at the test birth. *Pass: a test question walks in one door and out the same record.*
12. **The record** — every test state change lands in the Day-Book, timestamped and hash-chained, and the rehearsal's full evidence pack is filed as one document: links 1–11, each with its test record, its failures kept in (a rehearsal that hides its failures has not rehearsed). *Pass: the captain can re-walk any link from the filed evidence alone.*
13. **The founder's gate** — the rehearsal report goes to **the town's founder**. Charges go live only on his word after this report, the Part B notice's date, and the Q1 clearance are all documented. *Pass: the word is given, in the open, or the road stays closed.*
