01 · The Town

Before gold was money, salt was.

Provadia–Solnitsata, in today's Bulgaria, is Europe's oldest town. Est. 4700 BC.

Long before coins existed, the people there made money out of salt — baked into bricks, stacked, guarded, traded.

The salt works sat behind stone fortress walls — among the first stone fortifications in Europe — because the salt was worth guarding. It was the town's wealth, made by everyone, used by everyone.

Gold was for kings and graves. Salt was the people's money.

PROVADIA is named for that town and that fact: the first mint in Europe struck nothing — it baked salt. This page is that town, rebuilt online. The wall below is its square, and it is still being built.

“probably Europe's first ‘mint’”

— The site's lead archaeologist, on Provadia–Solnitsata

Legend — not fact

The word “salary” is said to have been born from salt. That is a story people tell about salt — told here as legend, never as fact.
