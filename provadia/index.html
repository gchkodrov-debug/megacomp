What is Provadia?

The first money in Europe was salt. Provadia (ticker: PROVADIA) is a fair-launch coin on Solana, named for the place where that money was born: Provadia–Solnitsata, a prehistoric town in today's Bulgaria — the oldest town in Europe — where, around 4700 BC, people produced salt, pressed it into bricks, and traded those bricks as money.

Every other coin invents a story. This one's story is in the archaeology books.

The place behind the name

  • The town: Provadia–Solnitsata, occupied from about 4700 to 4200 BC, built beside a brine spring — the only rock-salt source in the eastern Balkans.
  • The money: salt was wealth, food preservation, and currency in one. The town grew rich producing it and trading it as bricks.
  • The fortress: to guard the salt, its people built massive stone walls — among the first stone fortifications in Europe. Our coin's mark is that wall, and the letter P built from its bricks.
  • The gold: 40 km away lies Varna, where the world's oldest worked gold was buried (about 4600 BC). Salt and gold — the two oldest treasures — come from the same small corner of Bulgaria.
  • The verdict of science: lead archaeologist Prof. Vasil Nikolov calls the salt Europe's first form of money and the town probably Europe's first "mint." Probably is his word. We keep it.
