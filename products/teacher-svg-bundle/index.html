Teacher Cut-File & Classroom Decor SVG Pack — teacher SVG bundle

A classroom that looks cared for teaches before you say a word — and the fastest way to get there with a cutting machine is a set of files that were drawn to belong together. A good teacher SVG bundle is not five hundred random designs; it is the alphabet, the labels, the badges and the name tags in one matching hand, layered properly for two-colour cutting. That is the whole idea of this pack: 63 original designs, each supplied as SVG, DXF and transparent PNG, drawn as one set for bulletin boards and classroom decor.

The pain it solves is the mismatched download pile. A letter set from one seller, labels from another, badges in a third style — the board ends up looking assembled rather than designed, and half the files arrive flattened, unlayered, or with the counters of an A or an R filled in wrong. Every design in this pack is layered (a shadow or plate layer under a face layer) with the layers named when you upload to Cricut Design Space or Silhouette Studio, the letter counters cut properly, and the counts on this page are the counts in the folders — the Usage Guide counts the contents honestly, design by design.

What this pack is: digital cut files, drawn from original geometry by the maker's own builder script — no stock, no clipart, no traced artwork, and no characters, brands or logos anywhere in it. The letterforms are set in Liberation Sans, an open-licence font (SIL Open Font License), converted to paths. What it is not: nothing physical ships, and the files themselves are never yours to resell, share or upload — the licence covers the physical things you make with them, for your classroom and as finished items at craft-fair scale.

How does the layering work in Cricut or Silhouette?
The designs are layered for two-colour cutting: a shadow, plate or backing layer underneath and a face or word layer on top, and the layers arrive named when you upload to Cricut Design Space or Silhouette Studio, so you can assign each layer its material and colour. Letter counters — the holes in A, D, O, P, Q and R — are cut properly. The Usage Guide walks the workflow for both programs, including layer order and sizing.
What exactly does the licence allow?
In plain words: make physical items for your own classroom, and sell finished physical items at craft-fair scale — a board, a badge set, a set of name tags. What it never allows is reselling, sharing, giving away or uploading the digital files themselves, in whole or in part, modified or not. You are buying the right to make things with the files, not the right to redistribute them — the full licence is written in plain words in the Usage Guide.
